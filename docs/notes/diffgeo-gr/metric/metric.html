Chapter 7 • Metric

7.1 Metric

  • A metric \(g\) on a smooth manifold \((\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{A})\) is a \((0,2)\)-tensor field \begin{align*} g : \Gamma T\mathcal{M} \times \Gamma T\mathcal{M} &\xlongrightarrow{\substack{\sim \\ \sim}} C^\infty(\mathcal{M}) \\ (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) &\longmapsto g(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}), \end{align*} satisfying the following two conditions:

    1. Symmetry: \(g(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) = g(\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{X})\) for all \(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}\).

    2. Invertibility of the musical map: the musical map \begin{align*} \flat : \Gamma T\mathcal{M} &\longrightarrow \Gamma T^*\mathcal{M} \\ \mathcal{X} &\longmapsto \flat(\mathcal{X}), \qquad \text{with } \flat(\mathcal{X})\left\lceil \mathcal{Y} \right\rfloor \coloneqq g(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}), \end{align*} is an isomorphism of \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-modules. Explicitly, \(\flat\) must be

      1. (a)

        \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-linear: \(\flat(f \, \mathcal{X} + \mathcal{Y}) = f \, \flat(\mathcal{X}) + \flat(\mathcal{Y})\) for all \(f \in C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\) and \(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}\);

      2. (b)

        bijective: \(\forall \, \mathsf{X} \in \Gamma T^*\mathcal{M} \;\, \exists! \, \mathcal{X} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M} : \flat(\mathcal{X}) = \mathsf{X}\)1.

      That \(\flat(\mathcal{X})\) is indeed a covector field, and condition (a) follow from the \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-linearity of \(g\) in its second and first slot, respectively. The actual requirement is (b).

    A metric manifold is the quadruple \((\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{A}, g)\), i.e. a smooth manifold equipped with a metric.

  • In a chart \((\mathcal{U}, \mathscr{X})\), the components of the metric are the functions \[ g_{ij} \coloneqq g(\partial x_i, \partial x_j) \in C^\infty(\mathcal{U}), \] with \(g_{ij} = g_{ji}\) by symmetry. For \(\left. \mathcal{X} \right|_\mathcal{U} = x^i \, \partial x_i\) and \(\left. \mathcal{Y} \right|_\mathcal{U} = \tilde{x}^j \, \partial x_j\), \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-bilinearity gives \(\left. g(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) \right|_\mathcal{U} = x^i \, \tilde{x}^j \, g_{ij}\). When two charts are involved we write \((g_\mathscr{X})_{ij}\) and \((g_\mathscr{Y})_{kl}\); as for any \((0,2)\)-tensor field, on \(\mathcal{U} \cap \mathcal{V}\), \[ (g_\mathscr{Y})_{kl} = \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^i}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k} \, \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^j}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^l}\, (g_\mathscr{X})_{ij}. \]

  • Since \(\flat\) is an isomorphism, there exists the inverse musical map \begin{align*} \flat^{-1} : \Gamma T^*\mathcal{M} &\longrightarrow \Gamma T\mathcal{M} \\ \mathsf{X} &\longmapsto \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}), \end{align*} which is \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-linear as well (apply \(\flat^{-1}\) to \(\flat\big( f \, \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}) + \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{Y}) \big) = f \, \mathsf{X} + \mathsf{Y}\)). It defines the inverse metric \(g^{-1}\), the \((2,0)\)-tensor field \begin{align*} g^{-1} : \Gamma T^*\mathcal{M} \times \Gamma T^*\mathcal{M} &\xlongrightarrow{\substack{\sim \\ \sim}} C^\infty(\mathcal{M}) \\ (\mathsf{X}, \mathsf{Y}) &\longmapsto g^{-1}(\mathsf{X}, \mathsf{Y}) \coloneqq \mathsf{Y}\left\lceil \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}) \right\rfloor . \end{align*} It is symmetric: writing \(\mathsf{Y} = \flat\big( \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{Y}) \big)\) and using the symmetry of \(g\), \[ g^{-1}(\mathsf{X}, \mathsf{Y}) = \flat\big( \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{Y}) \big)\left\lceil \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}) \right\rfloor = g\big( \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{Y}), \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}) \big) = g\big( \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}), \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{Y}) \big) = g^{-1}(\mathsf{Y}, \mathsf{X}), \] which also shows that \(g^{-1}\) is simply \(g\) evaluated on the vector fields corresponding to \(\mathsf{X}\) and \(\mathsf{Y}\) under \(\flat^{-1}\). Its components in a chart are \((g^{-1})^{ij} \coloneqq g^{-1}(dx^i, dx^j) \in C^\infty(\mathcal{U})\).

    In components, the inverse metric is characterized by \(g_{ij} \, (g^{-1})^{jk} = \delta_i^k\)2. The matrix of components of \(g^{-1}\) is the inverse of the matrix of components of \(g\).

  • Musical maps in components. For \(\left. \mathcal{X} \right|_\mathcal{U} = x^j \, \partial x_j\) and \(\left. \mathsf{X} \right|_\mathcal{U} = \mathtt{x}_j \, dx^j\), the components of \(\flat(\mathcal{X}) \in \Gamma T^*\mathcal{M}\) and \(\flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}) \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}\) are \begin{align*} \flat(\mathcal{X})_i &\coloneqq \flat(\mathcal{X})\left\lceil \partial x_i \right\rfloor = g(x^i \partial x_i, \partial x_j) = x^j \, g_{ji},\\ \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X})^i &\coloneqq dx^i\left\lceil \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}) \right\rfloor = g^{-1}(\mathtt{x}_j dx^j, dx^i) = \mathtt{x}_j \, (g^{-1})^{ji}. \end{align*} This is what is usually called “lowering” and “raising” indices with \(g\) and \(g^{-1}\). In these notes the results always keep their own names, \(\flat(\mathcal{X})_i\) and \(\flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X})^i\), and are never written as \(x_i\) or \(\mathtt{x}^i\).

7.1.1 Signature

  • Unlike a \((1,1)\)-tensor field, \(g\) has no eigenvalues. For a \((1,1)\)-tensor \(T_p\) at \(p\), the eigenvalue equation in components, \[ T^i_j(p) \, x^j_p = \lambda \, x^i_p , \] equates the components of two vectors, \(T_p(\cdot, \mathcal{X}_p)\) and \(\lambda \, \mathcal{X}_p\). It is thus the component form of a chart-independent equation, and \(\lambda\) is the same in every chart. For the metric, the analogous equation \[ g_{ij}(p) \, x^j_p = \lambda \, x^i_p \] equates the components of the covector \(\flat_p(\mathcal{X}_p)\) with those of the vector \(\lambda \, \mathcal{X}_p\), which live in fundamentally different spaces. What survives is the so-called signature, as stated by Sylvester’s law of inertia (proved in Appendix B.12): at every \(p \in \mathcal{M}\) there exists a chart \((\mathcal{U}, \mathscr{X})\) with \(p \in \mathcal{U}\) whose components at \(p\) are \[ g_{ij}(p) = 0 \;\; \text{for } i \neq j, \qquad g_{ii}(p) \in \{+1, -1\}, \] i.e. in which the chart-induced basis \(\{ (\partial x_1)_p, \dots, (\partial x_d)_p \}\) of \(T_p\mathcal{M}\) “diagonalizes” \(g_p\), and the number \(n_+\) of \(+1\)’s (and hence the number \(n_-\) of \(-1\)’s) does not depend on the chart chosen. No zeros appear because \(g_p\) is non-degenerate3, so \(n_+ + n_- = \dim(\mathcal{M})\). The doublet \((n_+, n_-)\) is the signature of \(g\) at \(p\). It is, in fact, the same at all points of a connected manifold4, so we simply speak of the signature of \(g\). Metrics are classified according to it:

    1. If \(n_+ = \dim(\mathcal{M})\), the metric is Riemannian. It is then positive-definite, \[ g_p(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{X}_p) > 0 \qquad \forall \, \mathcal{X}_p \neq 0, \;\; \forall \, p \in \mathcal{M}, \] so each \(g_p\) is an inner product on \(T_p\mathcal{M}\). (If \(n_- = \dim(\mathcal{M})\), the metric is negative-definite and \(-g\) is Riemannian; this case is conventionally included.)

    2. If \(0 < n_+ < \dim(\mathcal{M})\), the metric is pseudo-Riemannian. Then there exist non-zero null vectors, \(g_p(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{X}_p) = 0\) with \(\mathcal{X}_p \neq 0\): if \(g_{aa}(p) = +1\) and \(g_{bb}(p) = -1\) in such a chart, take \(\mathcal{X}_p = (\partial x_a)_p + (\partial x_b)_p\). Each \(g_p\) is thus symmetric, bilinear and non-degenerate, but not positive-definite: a pseudo-inner product. In the special case \(n_+ = 1\) or \(n_- = 1\) (with \(\dim(\mathcal{M}) \geq 2\)) the metric is Lorentzian; both sign conventions, \((1, d-1)\) and \((d-1, 1)\), are common in the literature.

This can be checked pointwise. At each \(p\), \(\flat_p : T_p\mathcal{M} \to T_p^*\mathcal{M}\), \(\flat_p(\mathcal{X}_p)(\mathcal{Y}_p) \coloneqq g_p(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{Y}_p)\), with \(\flat(\mathcal{X})_p = \flat_p(\mathcal{X}_p)\). Since \(\dim T_p\mathcal{M} = \dim T_p^*\mathcal{M}\), (b) \(\iff\) (i) \(\flat_p\) is an isomorphism \(\forall \, p\) \(\iff\) (ii) \(\ker \flat_p = \{0\} \;\, \forall \, p\), i.e. \(g\) is non-degenerate: \(g_p(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{Y}_p) = 0 \;\, \forall \, \mathcal{Y}_p \Rightarrow \mathcal{X}_p = 0\) \(\iff\) (iii) \(\det\big( g_{ij}(p) \big) \neq 0 \;\, \forall \, p\), in every chart. (i) \(\Rightarrow\) (b): \(\flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X})_p \coloneqq \flat_p^{-1}(\mathsf{X}_p)\) is smooth, its components being \(\mathtt{x}_j\) times the entries of \(\big( g_{ij} \big)^{-1}\). (b) \(\Rightarrow\) (i): if \(\flat_{p_0}\) were singular, any \(\mathsf{Y}\) with \(\mathsf{Y}_{p_0} \notin \operatorname{im} \flat_{p_0}\) (constant components near \(p_0\) times a bump function) would have no preimage, as \(\flat(\mathcal{X})_{p_0} = \flat_{p_0}(\mathcal{X}_{p_0})\). Strictly weaker is injectivity alone, \(g(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) = 0 \;\, \forall \, \mathcal{Y} \Rightarrow \mathcal{X} = 0\): \(g = (\mathscr{X}^1)^2 \, dx^1 \otimes dx^1\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) satisfies it, yet \(g_0 = 0\) and \(dx^1 \notin \operatorname{im} \flat\), since \(\flat(x \, \partial x_1) = (\mathscr{X}^1)^2 \, x \, dx^1 = dx^1\) requires \(x = (\mathscr{X}^1)^{-2}\), not smooth at \(0\).

2

The name “inverse” does not mean that \(g^{-1}\) is the inverse of \(g\) as a map: \(g\) is a map from pairs of vector fields to scalar fields, so it has no inverse in that sense. It refers instead to the relation between the matrices of components, which follows from \(\flat^{-1}\) being the inverse of \(\flat\). Since \(\flat(\partial x_k)\left\lceil \partial x_l \right\rfloor = g_{kl}\) and \(dx^k\left\lceil \flat^{-1}(dx^j) \right\rfloor = (g^{-1})^{jk}\), we have \(\flat(\partial x_k) = g_{kl} \, dx^l\) and \(\flat^{-1}(dx^j) = (g^{-1})^{jk} \, \partial x_k\). Hence \(\partial x_i = \flat^{-1}\big( \flat(\partial x_i) \big) = g_{ij} \, (g^{-1})^{jk} \, \partial x_k\), and similarly \(dx^j = \flat\big( \flat^{-1}(dx^j) \big) = (g^{-1})^{jk} \, g_{kl} \, dx^l\), which, the chart-induced frames being bases, give \(g_{ij} \, (g^{-1})^{jk} = \delta_i{}^k\) and \((g^{-1})^{jk} \, g_{kl} = \delta^j{}_l\).

3

Sylvester’s law holds for any symmetric bilinear form on \(T_p\mathcal{M}\), degenerate or not: in a suitable basis it is diagonal with entries in \(\{+1, -1, 0\}\), and the numbers \((n_+, n_-, n_0)\) of each do not depend on the basis, with \(n_0 = \dim \ker \flat_p\). For a metric, \(\flat_p\) is an isomorphism, so \(n_0 = 0\). Appendix B.12 proves only the non-degenerate case.

4

In a chart, the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix \(\big( g_{ij}(p) \big)\) must depend continuously on \(p\). Since their product, \(\det\big( g_{ij}(p) \big)\), never vanishes, no eigenvalue can change sign, so the signature is locally constant, hence constant on a connected manifold.
