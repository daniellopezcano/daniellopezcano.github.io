Appendix B • Proofs & Comments

B.12 Sylvester’s law of inertia (§7.1.1)

Let \((V, \oplus, \odot)\) be an \(\mathbb{R}\)-vector space of dimension \(d \geq 1\) (we write \(+\) and juxtaposition for its operations) and let \begin{align*} b : V \times V &\xlongrightarrow{\substack{\sim \\ \sim}} \mathbb{R} \\ (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) &\longmapsto b(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) \end{align*} be a symmetric bilinear form which is non-degenerate: \(b(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) = 0\) for all \(\mathcal{Y} \in V\) implies \(\mathcal{X} = 0\). The case of interest in Chapter 7 is \(V = T_p\mathcal{M}\) and \(b = g_p\).

Statement.

  1. (i)

    Existence: there exists a basis \(\{ \hat{e}_1, \dots, \hat{e}_d \}\) of \(V\) such that \(b(\hat{e}_a, \hat{e}_b) = 0\) for \(a \neq b\) and \(b(\hat{e}_a, \hat{e}_a) \in \{+1, -1\}\).

  2. (ii)

    Uniqueness of the signs: the number \(n_+\) of basis vectors with \(b(\hat{e}_a, \hat{e}_a) = +1\), and hence the number \(n_- = d - n_+\) of those with \(b(\hat{e}_a, \hat{e}_a) = -1\), is the same for every basis as in (i).

Proof of (i), by induction on \(d\).

  • There is a vector \(\mathcal{X}_0\) with \(b(\mathcal{X}_0, \mathcal{X}_0) \neq 0\). Otherwise, by bilinearity and symmetry (polarization), \[ b(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) = \tfrac{1}{2} \Big[ b(\mathcal{X} + \mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{X} + \mathcal{Y}) - b(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{X}) - b(\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{Y}) \Big] = 0 \qquad \forall \, \mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y} \in V, \] contradicting non-degeneracy, since \(V \neq \{0\}\).

  • Normalization. Set \(\hat{e}_1 \coloneqq \mathcal{X}_0 / \sqrt{|b(\mathcal{X}_0, \mathcal{X}_0)|}\). Then \(b(\hat{e}_1, \hat{e}_1) = b(\mathcal{X}_0, \mathcal{X}_0) / |b(\mathcal{X}_0, \mathcal{X}_0)| = \pm 1\). If \(d = 1\), we are done.

  • Splitting off \(\hat{e}_1\). Let \(W \coloneqq \{ \mathcal{W} \in V \mid b(\hat{e}_1, \mathcal{W}) = 0 \}\), the kernel of the linear map \(b(\hat{e}_1, \cdot) : V \to \mathbb{R}\). This map is non-zero (it takes the value \(\pm 1\) on \(\hat{e}_1\)), hence surjective, and \(\dim W = d - 1\) by rank–nullity. Every \(\mathcal{X} \in V\) decomposes uniquely as \[ \mathcal{X} = \alpha \, \hat{e}_1 + \mathcal{W}, \qquad \alpha \coloneqq \frac{b(\hat{e}_1, \mathcal{X})}{b(\hat{e}_1, \hat{e}_1)}, \qquad \mathcal{W} \coloneqq \mathcal{X} - \alpha \, \hat{e}_1 \in W, \] where \(b(\hat{e}_1, \mathcal{W}) = b(\hat{e}_1, \mathcal{X}) - \alpha \, b(\hat{e}_1, \hat{e}_1) = 0\), and uniqueness follows by applying \(b(\hat{e}_1, \cdot)\) to two such decompositions.

  • \(b\) restricted to \(W\) is non-degenerate. If \(\mathcal{W}_0 \in W\) satisfies \(b(\mathcal{W}_0, \mathcal{W}) = 0\) for all \(\mathcal{W} \in W\), then for every \(\mathcal{X} = \alpha \, \hat{e}_1 + \mathcal{W}\), \[ b(\mathcal{W}_0, \mathcal{X}) = \alpha \, \underbrace{b(\mathcal{W}_0, \hat{e}_1)}_{= \, b(\hat{e}_1, \mathcal{W}_0) \, = \, 0} + \, b(\mathcal{W}_0, \mathcal{W}) = 0, \] so \(\mathcal{W}_0 = 0\) by non-degeneracy of \(b\) on \(V\).

  • Induction. Applying the induction hypothesis to \((W, b|_{W \times W})\) yields a basis \(\{ \hat{e}_2, \dots, \hat{e}_d \}\) of \(W\) with the required properties. Its elements satisfy \(b(\hat{e}_1, \hat{e}_a) = 0\) because they lie in \(W\), and by the unique decomposition above, \(\{ \hat{e}_1, \dots, \hat{e}_d \}\) is a basis of \(V\). \(\square\)

Proof of (ii). Let \(\{ \hat{e}_a \}\) and \(\{ \hat{e}'_a \}\) be two bases as in (i), with \(n_+\) and \(n'_+\) positive entries, respectively. Consider the subspaces \[ V_+ \coloneqq \operatorname{span}\{ \hat{e}_a \mid b(\hat{e}_a, \hat{e}_a) = +1 \}, \qquad V'_- \coloneqq \operatorname{span}\{ \hat{e}'_a \mid b(\hat{e}'_a, \hat{e}'_a) = -1 \}, \] of dimensions \(n_+\) and \(d - n'_+\). For a non-zero \(\mathcal{X} = \sum_a \alpha^a \, \hat{e}_a \in V_+\) (sum over the positive basis vectors only), \(b(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{X}) = \sum_a (\alpha^a)^2 > 0\), while for a non-zero \(\mathcal{Y} = \sum_a \beta^a \, \hat{e}'_a \in V'_-\), \(b(\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{Y}) = -\sum_a (\beta^a)^2 < 0\). Hence \(V_+ \cap V'_- = \{0\}\), and the dimension of the subspace \(V_+ + V'_- \subseteq V\) is \[ \dim(V_+ + V'_-) = \dim V_+ + \dim V'_- - \dim(V_+ \cap V'_-) = n_+ + (d - n'_+) \leq d \quad \longrightarrow \quad n_+ \leq n'_+ . \] Exchanging the roles of the two bases gives \(n'_+ \leq n_+\), so \(n_+ = n'_+\). \(\square\)

Remark (eigenvalues). Given any basis of \(V\), the matrix \(\mathbf{B}\) of entries \(b(\hat{x}_i, \hat{x}_j)\) is real and symmetric, so it can be diagonalized by an orthogonal matrix \(\mathbf{O}\), \(\mathbf{O}^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{B} \, \mathbf{O} = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_d)\). Since \(\mathbf{O}^{\mathsf{T}} = \mathbf{O}^{-1}\), this is at the same time a change of basis for \(b\) (a congruence), and rescaling the new basis vectors by \(1/\sqrt{|\lambda_a|}\) produces a basis as in (i). By (ii), \(n_+\) is therefore the number of positive eigenvalues of \(\mathbf{B}\), whichever basis was used to write it down. The eigenvalues themselves, however, do depend on the basis.

Remark (realization by a chart). In the case \(V = T_p\mathcal{M}\), \(b = g_p\), the basis of (i) can always be realized as the chart-induced basis at \(p\) of a suitable chart. Given any chart \((\mathcal{U}, \mathscr{X})\) around \(p\), write \(\hat{e}_a = A^i{}_a \, (\partial x_i)_p\), with \((A^i{}_a)\) an invertible matrix. The chart \[ \mathscr{Y}^a \coloneqq (A^{-1})^a{}_i \, \big( \mathscr{X}^i - \mathscr{X}^i(p) \big), \] obtained from \(\mathscr{X}\) by a linear (hence \(C^\infty\)-compatible) change of chart, satisfies \[ (\partial y_a)_p = A^i{}_a \, (\partial x_i)_p = \hat{e}_a , \] since \((\partial x_i)_p = \big( \partial \mathscr{Y}^a / \partial \mathscr{X}^i \big)_p \, (\partial y_a)_p = (A^{-1})^a{}_i \, (\partial y_a)_p\). In this chart, the components of the metric at \(p\) are diagonal, \(g_{ab}(p) = 0\) for \(a \neq b\), with \(g_{aa}(p) = \pm 1\).
