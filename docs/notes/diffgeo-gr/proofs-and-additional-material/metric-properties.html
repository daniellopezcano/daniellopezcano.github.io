B.18 Properties of \(g\) and of the L.C. connection (§§7.3–7.4)

Appendix B • Proofs & Comments

B.18 Properties of \(g\) and of the L.C. connection (§§7.3–7.4)

Throughout, \((\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{A}, g)\) is a metric manifold of dimension \(d\) and arbitrary signature, \((\mathcal{U}, \mathscr{X})\) is a chart, the Einstein summation convention is implied, and \(\partial_k F \coloneqq \partial x_k \left\lfloor F \right\rceil\) for \(F \in C^\infty(\mathcal{U})\), so that \(\partial_k \partial_j F\) are ordinary second partial derivatives of \(F \circ \mathscr{X}^{-1}\). Components of covariant derivatives along chart-induced basis fields, \((\nabla_{\partial x_k} T)^{i \dots}_{j \dots}\), follow the rule of Chapter 6: one \(+\Gamma\) term per upper index, one \(-\Gamma\) term per lower index. From Subsection B.18.5 on, \(\nabla = \nabla^{\text{LC}}\), \(\Gamma^k_{ji} = \big( \Gamma^{\text{LC}}_\mathscr{X} \big)^k_{ji}\), and \(\mathrm{Riem}\), \(\mathrm{Ric}\) are its curvatures.

B.18.1 Derivative of the inverse metric

\[ \boxed{ \partial_c (g^{-1})^{ab} = - (g^{-1})^{ar} \, (g^{-1})^{bs} \, \partial_c g_{rs} . } \] Proof. Apply \(\partial x_c\) to the constant function \((g^{-1})^{ar} \, g_{rs} = \delta^a_s\) and use the Leibniz rule: \(0 = \partial_c (g^{-1})^{ar} \; g_{rs} + (g^{-1})^{ar} \; \partial_c g_{rs}\). Multiplying by \((g^{-1})^{sb}\), with \(g_{rs} \, (g^{-1})^{sb} = \delta^b_r\), and using the symmetry of \(g^{-1}\) gives the claim. \(\square\)

B.18.2 Derivative of the determinant and contracted Christoffel symbols

Let \(\det g \coloneqq \det\big( g_{ij} \big) \in C^\infty(\mathcal{U})\), which never vanishes. Then \[ \boxed{ \partial_k \det g = \det g \; (g^{-1})^{ij} \, \partial_k g_{ij}, \qquad \big( \Gamma^{\text{LC}}_\mathscr{X} \big)^m_{mk} = \partial_k \ln \sqrt{|\det g|} . } \] Proof. For \(\mathbf{A}\) invertible, \(\det(\mathbf{A} + t \, \mathbf{B}) = \det \mathbf{A} \, \det\big( \mathbf{I} + t \, \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{B} \big) = \det \mathbf{A} \, \big( 1 + t \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{B}) + O(t^2) \big)\): in the sum over permutations, every permutation other than the identity picks at least two off-diagonal entries, each of order \(t\). Along the \(k\)-th coordinate line through \(q\), the matrix of components is \(\mathbf{A} + t \, \mathbf{B} + O(t^2)\), with \(\mathbf{A} = \big( g_{ij}(q) \big)\) and \(\mathbf{B} = \big( \partial_k g_{ij}(q) \big)\), and the derivative at \(t = 0\) gives the first formula (Jacobi’s formula). For the second, contracting the Christoffel symbols, \[ \big( \Gamma^{\text{LC}}_\mathscr{X} \big)^m_{mk} = \frac{1}{2} (g^{-1})^{ml} \big( \partial_k g_{lm} + \partial_m g_{lk} - \partial_l g_{mk} \big) = \frac{1}{2} (g^{-1})^{ml} \, \partial_k g_{lm} = \frac{\partial_k \det g}{2 \det g}, \] where the last two terms in the bracket cancel after exchanging the dummy indices \(m \leftrightarrow l\) in one of them. \(\square\)

The density \(\sqrt{|\det g|}\) reappears in the chapter on integration.

B.18.3 Covariant derivatives commute with contractions

For any connection, contracting an upper with a lower index commutes with covariant differentiation. For \(\mathrm{Ric}_{jm} = \mathrm{Riem}^k_{jkm}\), \[ \boxed{ (\nabla_{\partial x_n} \mathrm{Riem})^k_{jkm} = (\nabla_{\partial x_n} \mathrm{Ric})_{jm} , } \] and likewise for any other contraction. Proof. By the component rule, \[ (\nabla_{\partial x_n} \mathrm{Riem})^k_{jkm} = \partial_n \mathrm{Riem}^k_{jkm} + \underbrace{\Gamma^k_{ln} \, \mathrm{Riem}^l_{jkm}}_{(\ast)} - \Gamma^l_{jn} \, \mathrm{Riem}^k_{lkm} - \underbrace{\Gamma^l_{kn} \, \mathrm{Riem}^k_{jlm}}_{(\ast\ast)} - \Gamma^l_{mn} \, \mathrm{Riem}^k_{jkl} , \] and exchanging the dummy indices \(k \leftrightarrow l\) in \((\ast\ast)\) turns it into \((\ast)\). The remaining terms are the component rule for the \((0,2)\)-tensor field \(\mathrm{Ric}\). \(\square\)

B.18.4 Moving the metric through the covariant derivative

For any connection, let \(T\) be a \((1,1)\)-tensor field and feed its covector slot with \(\flat\): \[ (g \cdot T)(\mathcal{Z}, \mathcal{Y}) \coloneqq T\big( \flat(\mathcal{Z}), \mathcal{Y} \big), \qquad (g \cdot T)_{ij} = T(g_{im} \, dx^m, \partial x_j) = g_{im} \, T^m_j , \] using \(\flat(\partial x_i) = g_{im} \, dx^m\). Then the Leibniz rule holds for this contraction, \[ \boxed{ \big( \nabla_{\partial x_k} (g \cdot T) \big)_{ij} = (\nabla_{\partial x_k} g)_{im} \, T^m_j + g_{im} \, (\nabla_{\partial x_k} T)^m_j . } \] Proof. By the component rule, \[ \begin{aligned} \big( \nabla_{\partial x_k} (g \cdot T) \big)_{ij} &= \partial_k \big( g_{im} T^m_j \big) - \Gamma^n_{ik} \, g_{nm} T^m_j - \Gamma^n_{jk} \, g_{im} T^m_n , \\ g_{im} (\nabla_{\partial x_k} T)^m_j &= g_{im} \, \partial_k T^m_j + g_{in} \, \Gamma^n_{mk} \, T^m_j - \Gamma^n_{jk} \, g_{im} T^m_n , \end{aligned} \] where \(m \leftrightarrow n\) has been relabelled in the middle term of the second line. Subtracting, the terms with \(\partial_k T^m_j\) and \(\Gamma^n_{jk}\) cancel, and by (7.3) \[ \big( \nabla_{\partial x_k} (g \cdot T) \big)_{ij} - g_{im} (\nabla_{\partial x_k} T)^m_j = \big( \partial_k g_{im} - \Gamma^n_{ik} \, g_{nm} - \Gamma^n_{mk} \, g_{in} \big) T^m_j = (\nabla_{\partial x_k} g)_{im} \, T^m_j . \qquad \square \] The same computation applies to every slot of a tensor field of any valence fed with \(\flat\), each contributing one term with \(\nabla g\), and to every slot fed with \(\flat^{-1}\), each contributing one term with \(\nabla g^{-1}\). For \(T = \mathcal{Y}\), a \((1,0)\)-tensor field, \((g \cdot \mathcal{Y})_i = g_{im} \, y^m = \flat(\mathcal{Y})_i\).

B.18.5 The Levi-Civita connection preserves the metric

From here on, \(\nabla = \nabla^{\text{LC}}\). By (7.3), \(\nabla g = 0\) reads \begin{equation} \partial_k g_{ij} = \Gamma^m_{ik} \, g_{mj} + \Gamma^m_{jk} \, g_{im} . \tag{B.4} \end{equation}

  1. The inverse metric is parallel, \((\nabla_{\partial x_k} g^{-1})^{ij} = 0\). By the component rule, Subsection B.18.1 and (B.4), \begin{align*} (\nabla_{\partial x_k} g^{-1})^{ij} &= \partial_k (g^{-1})^{ij} + \Gamma^i_{mk} \, (g^{-1})^{mj} + \Gamma^j_{mk} \, (g^{-1})^{im} \\ &= - (g^{-1})^{ir} (g^{-1})^{js} \big( \Gamma^m_{rk} \, g_{ms} + \Gamma^m_{sk} \, g_{rm} \big) + \Gamma^i_{mk} \, (g^{-1})^{mj} + \Gamma^j_{mk} \, (g^{-1})^{im} \\ &= - \Gamma^j_{rk} \, (g^{-1})^{ir} - \Gamma^i_{sk} \, (g^{-1})^{js} + \Gamma^i_{mk} \, (g^{-1})^{mj} + \Gamma^j_{mk} \, (g^{-1})^{im} = 0 . \end{align*}

  2. The metric moves through \(\nabla\). By Subsection B.18.4, \(\nabla g = 0\) and item 1, contracting with \(g\) or \(g^{-1}\) commutes with \(\nabla\), e.g. \(\nabla_\mathcal{X} (g \cdot T) = g \cdot \nabla_\mathcal{X} T\). In particular, \(\nabla_\mathcal{X} \flat(\mathcal{Y}) = \flat(\nabla_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y})\) and \(\nabla_\mathcal{X} \flat^{-1}(\mathsf{X}) = \flat^{-1}(\nabla_\mathcal{X} \mathsf{X})\).

  3. Parallel transport preserves \(g\). For vector fields \(\mathcal{X}_\gamma\), \(\mathcal{Y}_\gamma\) along \(\gamma\), with component functions \(x^a_\gamma\), \(y^b_\gamma\), and all functions on \(\mathcal{U}\) evaluated at \(\gamma(\lambda)\), the chart expression \((\ast\ast)\) of Chapter 6 gives \[ \begin{aligned} g\big( \nabla_{v_\gamma} \mathcal{X}_\gamma, \mathcal{Y}_\gamma \big) + g\big( \mathcal{X}_\gamma, \nabla_{v_\gamma} \mathcal{Y}_\gamma \big) &= g_{ab} \Big( \frac{d x^a_\gamma}{d\lambda} \, y^b_\gamma + x^a_\gamma \, \frac{d y^b_\gamma}{d\lambda} \Big) + \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \, x^j_\gamma \, y^b_\gamma \underbrace{\big( \Gamma^a_{ji} \, g_{ab} + \Gamma^a_{bi} \, g_{ja} \big)}_{\partial_i g_{jb} \text{ by~(B.4)}} \\ &= \frac{d}{d\lambda} \, g\big( \mathcal{X}_\gamma, \mathcal{Y}_\gamma \big), \end{aligned} \] by the product rule. Parallelly transported fields therefore keep their magnitudes and mutual angles. For \(\mathcal{X}_\gamma = \mathcal{Y}_\gamma = v_\gamma\) along an autoparallelly transported curve, \(\frac{d}{d\lambda} \, g(v_\gamma, v_\gamma) = 2 \, g(\nabla_{v_\gamma} v_\gamma, v_\gamma) = 0\): such curves have constant \(\textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma\) and \(s_\gamma\).

B.18.6 The Riemann–Christoffel curvature in normal coordinates

Let \((\mathcal{U}, \mathscr{X})\) be a normal chart at \(p\) (Chapter 6). Since \(\nabla^{\text{LC}}\) is torsion free, all its connection coefficient functions vanish at \(p\), and then, by (B.4), so do all first derivatives of the metric, \(\partial_k g_{ij}(p) = 0\). In such a chart, \[ \boxed{ (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm}(p) = \frac{1}{2} \Big( \partial_k \partial_j g_{am} - \partial_k \partial_a g_{jm} - \partial_m \partial_j g_{ak} + \partial_m \partial_a g_{jk} \Big)(p) . } \] Proof. At \(p\) the quadratic terms of \(\mathrm{Riem}^i_{jkm} = \partial_k \Gamma^i_{jm} - \partial_m \Gamma^i_{jk} + \Gamma^i_{rk} \, \Gamma^r_{jm} - \Gamma^i_{rm} \, \Gamma^r_{jk}\) vanish, and in \(\partial_k \Gamma^i_{jm}\) the term containing \(\partial_k (g^{-1})^{il}\) multiplies first derivatives of \(g\), so \[ \partial_k \Gamma^i_{jm}(p) = \frac{1}{2} (g^{-1})^{il} \big( \partial_k \partial_m g_{lj} + \partial_k \partial_j g_{lm} - \partial_k \partial_l g_{jm} \big)(p). \] Subtracting the same expression with \(k \leftrightarrow m\), the terms \(\partial_k \partial_m g_{lj}\) cancel by Schwarz’s theorem, and \((\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm} = g_{ai} \, \mathrm{Riem}^i_{jkm}\), with \(g_{ai} (g^{-1})^{il} = \delta^l_a\), gives the claim. \(\square\)

B.18.7 Symmetries of the Riemann–Christoffel curvature

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} &\text{(a)} \quad (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm} = - (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajmk}, &&\text{(b)} \quad (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm} = - (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{jakm}, \\ &\text{(c)} \quad (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm} + (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{akmj} + (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{amjk} = 0, &&\text{(d)} \quad (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm} = (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{kmaj}. \end{aligned} } \] (a) holds for any connection, (c) is the algebraic Bianchi identity, (b) and (d) are specific to \(\nabla^{\text{LC}}\). Proof. Each identity states that a \((0,4)\)-tensor field vanishes, so it suffices to check it at an arbitrary \(p\) in a normal chart at \(p\). With \(\partial_{km} g_{aj} \coloneqq \partial_k \partial_m g_{aj}(p)\), symmetric in \(k, m\) and in \(a, j\), \[ 2 \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm}(p) = \partial_{kj} g_{am} - \partial_{ka} g_{jm} - \partial_{mj} g_{ak} + \partial_{ma} g_{jk} . \] (a): \(k \leftrightarrow m\) maps the four terms onto minus themselves. (b): \(a \leftrightarrow j\) swaps the first two and the last two terms, flipping the sign. (d): \((a,j) \leftrightarrow (k,m)\) gives \(\partial_{am} g_{kj} - \partial_{ak} g_{mj} - \partial_{jm} g_{ka} + \partial_{jk} g_{ma}\), the same four terms. (c): the three cyclic permutations of \((j,k,m)\), \[ \begin{aligned} &\partial_{kj} g_{am} - \partial_{ka} g_{jm} - \partial_{mj} g_{ak} + \partial_{ma} g_{jk} \\ + \, &\partial_{mk} g_{aj} - \partial_{ma} g_{kj} - \partial_{jk} g_{am} + \partial_{ja} g_{km} \\ + \, &\partial_{jm} g_{ak} - \partial_{ja} g_{mk} - \partial_{km} g_{aj} + \partial_{ka} g_{mj} = 0 , \end{aligned} \] cancel term by term. \(\square\)

Multiplying (c) by \((g^{-1})^{ia}\), \(\mathrm{Riem}^i_{jkm} + \mathrm{Riem}^i_{kmj} + \mathrm{Riem}^i_{mjk} = 0\). Together, (a)–(d) leave \(d^2 (d^2 - 1)/12\) independent component functions (not proved here): one for \(d = 2\), six for \(d = 3\), twenty for \(d = 4\).

B.18.8 Symmetry of the Ricci and Einstein curvatures

\[ \boxed{ \mathrm{Ric}_{ab} = \mathrm{Ric}_{ba}, \qquad G_{ab} = G_{ba} . } \] Proof. With \(\mathrm{Riem}^m_{amb} = (g^{-1})^{mc} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{camb}\), (d), the relabelling \(m \leftrightarrow c\) and the symmetry of \(g^{-1}\), \[ \mathrm{Ric}_{ab} = (g^{-1})^{mc} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{camb} = (g^{-1})^{mc} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{mbca} = (g^{-1})^{cm} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{cbma} = \mathrm{Ric}_{ba} . \] \(G = \mathrm{Ric} - \frac{1}{2} R \, g\) is then a sum of symmetric tensor fields. \(\square\)

B.18.9 Two dimensions

If \(d = 2\), the curvature is determined by the Ricci scalar: \[ \boxed{ (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ajkm} = \frac{R}{2} \big( g_{ak} \, g_{jm} - g_{am} \, g_{jk} \big), \qquad \mathrm{Ric} = \frac{R}{2} \, g, \qquad G = 0 . } \] Proof. By (a) and (b), the only non-zero components are \[ \rho \coloneqq (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{1212} = (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{2121} = -(\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{1221} = -(\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{2112} . \] With \(\mathrm{Ric}_{ab} = (g^{-1})^{mc} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{camb}\) and \((g^{-1})^{11} = g_{22} / \det g\), \((g^{-1})^{12} = -g_{12} / \det g\), \((g^{-1})^{22} = g_{11} / \det g\), \[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{Ric}_{11} &= (g^{-1})^{22} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{2121} = \frac{\rho}{\det g} \, g_{11}, \\ \mathrm{Ric}_{12} &= (g^{-1})^{12} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{2112} = \frac{\rho}{\det g} \, g_{12}, \\ \mathrm{Ric}_{22} &= (g^{-1})^{11} \, (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{1212} = \frac{\rho}{\det g} \, g_{22}. \end{aligned} \] Contracting, \(R = (g^{-1})^{mk} \mathrm{Ric}_{km} = 2 \rho / \det g\), so \(\mathrm{Ric} = \frac{R}{2} g\) and \(G = 0\). The right-hand side of the first formula has the symmetries (a), (b) and \(1212\) component \(\frac{R}{2} \det g = \rho\). \(\square\)

B.18.10 The contracted Bianchi identity

\[ \boxed{ (g^{-1})^{kl} \, (\nabla_{\partial x_k} G)_{ln} = 0 . } \] Proof. Contracting \(i = k\) in the differential Bianchi identity of Chapter 6, \[ (\nabla_{\partial x_n} \mathrm{Riem})^i_{jkm} + (\nabla_{\partial x_k} \mathrm{Riem})^i_{jmn} + (\nabla_{\partial x_m} \mathrm{Riem})^i_{jnk} = 0 , \] and using Subsection B.18.3 and (a), \[ (\nabla_{\partial x_n} \mathrm{Ric})_{jm} + (\nabla_{\partial x_k} \mathrm{Riem})^k_{jmn} - (\nabla_{\partial x_m} \mathrm{Ric})_{jn} = 0 . \] Multiply by \((g^{-1})^{jm}\), which commutes with \(\nabla\) (Subsection B.18.5). The first term becomes \(\partial_n R\). For the second, by (b) and (d), \((g^{-1})^{jm} (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{ljmn} = - (g^{-1})^{jm} (\mathrm{Riem}^\flat)_{mnjl} = - \mathrm{Ric}_{nl}\), so it becomes \(-(g^{-1})^{kl} (\nabla_{\partial x_k} \mathrm{Ric})_{nl}\), and with the symmetry of \(\mathrm{Ric}\), \(\partial_n R - 2 \, (g^{-1})^{kl} (\nabla_{\partial x_k} \mathrm{Ric})_{ln} = 0\). Finally, \(\nabla g = 0\) gives \((\nabla_{\partial x_k} (R \, g))_{ln} = \partial_k R \; g_{ln}\), so \[ (g^{-1})^{kl} (\nabla_{\partial x_k} G)_{ln} = \frac{1}{2} \partial_n R - \frac{1}{2} (g^{-1})^{kl} \, g_{ln} \, \partial_k R = 0 . \qquad \square \]

B.18.11 The energy Lagrangian

The square root in \(\mathcal{L}\) (Chapter 7) can be dropped. The energy Lagrangian is \begin{align*} \mathcal{T} : T\mathcal{M} &\longrightarrow \mathbb{R} \\ \mathcal{X}_p &\longmapsto \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{X}_p) \coloneqq g_p(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{X}_p), \qquad \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} \coloneqq \mathcal{T} \circ \xi_\mathscr{X}^{-1} : (\alpha \, ; \beta) \longmapsto \big( g_{ij} \circ \mathscr{X}^{-1} \big)(\alpha) \, \beta^i \beta^j , \end{align*} so that \(\mathcal{L} = \sqrt{\textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma \, \mathcal{T}}\) where \(\mathcal{L}\) is defined.

  1. Its Euler–Lagrange equations are (7.1). With \(\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} = 2 \, (g_{mj} \circ \mathscr{X}^{-1}) \, \beta^j\) and \(\partial_m \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} = \partial_m (g_{ij} \circ \mathscr{X}^{-1}) \, \beta^i \beta^j\) on \((\gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U})\), \[ \frac{d}{d\lambda} \big( 2 \, g_{mj} \, \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} \big) - \partial_m g_{ij} \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} = 2 \, g_{mj} \, \ddot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} + \big( \partial_i g_{mj} + \partial_j g_{mi} - \partial_m g_{ij} \big) \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} , \] splitting \(2 \, \partial_i g_{mj} \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U}\) symmetrically in \(i, j\). Setting it to zero and multiplying by \(\frac{1}{2} (g^{-1})^{km}\) gives (7.1). In practice this is the fastest way of computing the Christoffel symbols: read them off as the coefficients of \(\dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U}\).

  2. Its solutions have constant \(\mathcal{T}\). Along a solution, \[ \frac{d}{d\lambda} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} = \partial_m \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} \, \dot{\gamma}^m_\mathcal{U} + \partial_{d+m} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} \, \ddot{\gamma}^m_\mathcal{U} = \frac{d}{d\lambda} \big( \partial_{d+m} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} \, \dot{\gamma}^m_\mathcal{U} \big) = \frac{d}{d\lambda} \big( 2 \, \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} \big), \] since \(\beta^m \partial_{d+m} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} = 2 \, \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X}\). Hence \(\frac{d}{d\lambda} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} = 0\).

  3. Relation with \(\mathcal{L}\). Where \(\textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma \neq 0\), \(\partial \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X} = \textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma \, \partial \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} / (2 \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\) for every slot, and \(\mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X} = s_\gamma\) along the curve, so \[ \frac{d}{d\lambda} \big[ \partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X} \big] - \partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X} = \frac{\textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma}{2 s_\gamma} \Big( \frac{d}{d\lambda} \big[ \partial_{d+m} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} \big] - \partial_m \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} - \frac{s_\gamma'}{s_\gamma} \, \partial_{d+m} \mathcal{T}_\mathscr{X} \Big). \] For constant speed both sets of equations coincide, and by item 2 every solution for \(\mathcal{T}\) has constant speed. The solutions for \(\mathcal{T}\) are thus the constant-speed geodesics and the null geodesics (\(\mathcal{T} = 0\), where \(\mathcal{L}\) is not differentiable). The price is that \(\int \mathcal{T}\), unlike \(L_{[a,b]}\), depends on the parametrization.
