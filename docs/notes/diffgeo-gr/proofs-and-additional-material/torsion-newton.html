B.22 Torsion in Newton-Cartan Theory (§9.1)

Appendix B • Proofs & Comments

B.22 Torsion in Newton-Cartan Theory (§9.1)

Two statements about torsion are frequently encountered, and seem to contradict each other: “torsion does not affect the autoparallels, since the autoparallel equation only involves the symmetric part of the connection coefficient functions”, and “torsion does affect the autoparallels”. Both are correct. They differ in what is held fixed when torsion is introduced: either the symmetric part of the connection coefficient functions, or some other structure from which the connection is constructed, such as a metric. Torsion is written as in Chapter 6, \(\mathtt{T}^i_{jk} = \Gamma^i_{kj} - \Gamma^i_{jk}\), with \(\Gamma^i_{jk} = dx^i\left\lceil \nabla_{\partial x_k} \partial x_j \right\rfloor\). Even when torsion leaves the autoparallels unchanged, it does affect parallel transport and curvature, which is why the torsion-free condition in the definition of Newtonian spacetime matters (Subsections B.22.4 and B.22.5).

B.22.1 Statement A: the Symmetric Part is Held Fixed

  • Every connection coefficient function splits into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts in the two lower indices, \begin{align*} \Gamma^i_{jk} = \Gamma^i_{(jk)} + \Gamma^i_{[jk]}, \qquad \Gamma^i_{[jk]} \coloneqq \frac{1}{2} \Big[ \Gamma^i_{jk} - \Gamma^i_{kj} \Big] = - \frac{1}{2} \, \mathtt{T}^i_{jk}, \end{align*} so the antisymmetric part is the torsion, up to a factor \(-1/2\), while the symmetric part is independent of it. Since in the autoparallel equation \begin{align*} \ddot{\gamma}^k_\mathcal{U} + \Gamma^k_{ji} \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \, \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} = \ddot{\gamma}^k_\mathcal{U} + \Gamma^k_{(ji)} \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \, \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} = 0 \end{align*} the coefficient functions are contracted with the symmetric product \(\dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U}\), the antisymmetric part drops out.

  • Statement A. Two connections with the same symmetric part, \(\tilde{\Gamma}^k_{(ij)} = \Gamma^k_{(ij)}\), have the same autoparallels, whatever their torsions. Without charts: the difference of two connections is a \((1,2)\)-tensor field \(S\) (Chapter 6), and both connections have the same autoparallels if and only if \(S\) is antisymmetric in its two vector slots, i.e. if they differ only by torsion.

  • This is the right statement whenever the connection itself is the primary structure, freely specified by its coefficient functions, as in the Newtonian spacetime of Chapter 9.

B.22.2 Statement B: a Metric is Held Fixed

  • In most physical theories the connection is not specified freely but constructed from other structures, subject to compatibility conditions. The standard case is a metric: a symmetric \((0,2)\)-tensor field \(g\) whose matrix of component functions \(g_{ij} \coloneqq g(\partial x_i, \partial x_j)\) is invertible at every point, with inverse \(g^{ij}\), \(g^{ik} g_{kj} = \delta^i_j\). A connection is metric compatible if \(\nabla g = 0\), i.e. if parallel transport preserves the lengths of and angles between vectors. Writing \(\Gamma_{kij} \coloneqq g_{km} \Gamma^m_{ij}\) and \(\mathtt{T}_{kij} \coloneqq g_{km} \mathtt{T}^m_{ij}\), the condition reads \begin{align*} 0 = \big( \nabla_{\partial x_k} g \big)(\partial x_i, \partial x_j) = \partial x_k\left\lfloor g_{ij} \right\rceil - \Gamma_{jik} - \Gamma_{ijk}. \end{align*}

  • Given \(g\) and a prescribed torsion \(\mathtt{T}\), there is exactly one metric compatible connection with that torsion10: \begin{align*} \Gamma_{kij} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} \Big[ \partial x_i\left\lfloor g_{jk} \right\rceil + \partial x_j\left\lfloor g_{ki} \right\rceil - \partial x_k\left\lfloor g_{ij} \right\rceil \Big]}_{\eqqcolon \, \mathring{\Gamma}_{kij}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} \Big[ \mathtt{T}_{kji} + \mathtt{T}_{jki} + \mathtt{T}_{ikj} \Big]}_{\eqqcolon \, K_{kij}}. \end{align*} The first term is the Levi-Civita connection, the unique torsion-free metric compatible one, symmetric in \(i, j\); the second is the contorsion, linear in the torsion. Its antisymmetric part reproduces the torsion, \(K_{kij} - K_{kji} = - \mathtt{T}_{kij}\), but its symmetric part does not vanish in general: \begin{align*} \Gamma^k_{(ij)} = \mathring{\Gamma}^k_{ij} + \frac{1}{2} \, g^{km} \Big[ \mathtt{T}_{imj} + \mathtt{T}_{jmi} \Big]. \end{align*}

  • Statement B. For a fixed metric, the autoparallels of the metric compatible connection with torsion \(\mathtt{T}\) satisfy \begin{align*} \ddot{\gamma}^k_\mathcal{U} + \mathring{\Gamma}^k_{ji} \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \, \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} + g^{km} \, \mathtt{T}_{imj} \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \, \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U} = 0, \end{align*} so torsion does affect them. The additional term vanishes for all velocities if and only if \(\mathtt{T}_{imj} = - \mathtt{T}_{jmi}\), which, combined with the antisymmetry of torsion in its last two indices, means that \(\mathtt{T}_{kij}\) is totally antisymmetric. Only then are the autoparallels those of the Levi-Civita connection.

B.22.3 Conclusion

The two statements describe different families of connections:

  1. (A)

    fixing \(\Gamma^k_{(ij)}\) and varying \(\mathtt{T}\) leaves the autoparallels unchanged, but in general destroys metric compatibility;

  2. (B)

    fixing \(g\) and \(\nabla g = 0\) and varying \(\mathtt{T}\) preserves metric compatibility, but in general changes \(\Gamma^k_{(ij)}\), hence the autoparallels.

They agree precisely for totally antisymmetric torsion. Asking whether torsion affects autoparallels is therefore not meaningful until the structure from which the connection is built is specified.

B.22.4 Torsion Beyond Autoparallels

  • Even in case (A), torsion is not invisible to the connection: it affects the parallel transport of vectors other than the velocity. Along a curve \(\gamma\), a vector field \(\mathcal{X}_\gamma\) with components \(x^k_\gamma\) is parallel transported if \begin{align*} \frac{d x^k_\gamma}{d\lambda} + \Gamma^k_{ji} \, x^j_\gamma \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} = 0, \end{align*} where \(\Gamma\) is contracted with two different vectors, so its antisymmetric part contributes. Only for \(\mathcal{X}_\gamma = v_\gamma\), which yields the autoparallel equation, does it drop out. Since the curvature measures the failure of parallel transport around closed curves, \(\mathrm{Riem}\) and \(\mathrm{Ric}\) depend on the torsion as well: if \(\tilde{\nabla}\) and \(\nabla\) differ only by torsion, they have the same autoparallels but, in general, different curvatures.

  • Example. In a stratified chart of a Newtonian spacetime with all \(\Gamma^i_{jk} = 0\), i.e. without gravity, add \(\tilde{\Gamma}^\alpha_{\beta\delta} \coloneqq \varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\delta}\), the totally antisymmetric symbol with \(\varepsilon_{123} = 1\). The new connection still satisfies \(\nabla dt = 0\), its torsion is \(\mathtt{T}^\alpha_{\beta\delta} = - 2 \, \varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\delta}\), and its autoparallels are unchanged: free particles move along straight lines at constant velocity \(u^\alpha\). A spatial vector transported along such a worldline, however, obeys \(\frac{d x^\alpha_\gamma}{dt} = - \varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\delta} \, x^\beta_\gamma \, u^\delta\): it rotates about the direction of motion. Correspondingly, by the component expression of \(\mathrm{Riem}\) (Chapter 6), \begin{align*} \widetilde{\mathrm{Riem}}^\alpha_{\beta\delta\kappa} = \delta_{\alpha\kappa} \, \delta_{\beta\delta} - \delta_{\alpha\delta} \, \delta_{\beta\kappa}, \qquad \widetilde{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\beta\kappa} = - 2 \, \delta_{\beta\kappa}: \end{align*} a spacetime with no gravity, in which every free particle moves exactly as in the torsion-free case, has non-vanishing curvature, and even violates the vacuum field equation \(\mathrm{Ric} = 0\).

  • Torsion also enters two further results of Chapter 6. A chart with all \(\Gamma^i_{jk} = 0\) exists only if both \(\mathrm{Riem}\) and \(\mathtt{T}\) vanish, since torsion, being a tensor field, cannot be removed by any choice of chart. And the identification of the relative acceleration of neighbouring autoparallels with the curvature, as in the tidal acceleration of Section 9.4, acquires torsion terms, so that with torsion it no longer equals \(\mathrm{Riem}\).

B.22.5 The Newtonian Case

  • Uniform flow restricts torsion even before choosing the connection. The derivation \(\big( \nabla_{\partial x_i} dt \big)_k = - \Gamma^0_{ki}\) of Section 9.3 does not use torsion-freeness, so \(\nabla dt = 0\) gives \(\Gamma^0_{ki} = 0\) for all \(k, i\), and hence \(\mathtt{T}^0_{jk} = 0\): the torsion of any connection compatible with absolute time is spatial.

  • Within Newton’s axioms, torsion is a gauge freedom. The axioms of Section 9.2 only involve worldlines, i.e. velocities transported along themselves. Free particles therefore determine \(\Gamma^k_{(ij)}\), since knowing \(\Gamma^k_{(ij)} \, \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U} \, \dot{\gamma}^j_\mathcal{U}\) for all velocities fixes the symmetric part, but leave the torsion completely free, and no prediction depends on it. Requiring \(\mathtt{T} = 0\) selects the unique connection determined by free particles. It is a convention, but a necessary one: only for this connection do \(\mathrm{Riem}\) and \(\mathrm{Ric}\) depend on observable quantities alone, so that \(\mathrm{Riem} \neq 0\) signals gravity, \(\mathrm{Ric} = 4 \pi G \rho \; dt\left\lceil \cdot \right\rfloor dt\left\lceil \cdot \right\rfloor\) ties curvature to matter, and \(\mathrm{Riem}^\alpha_{0\beta0}\) is the tidal acceleration. With torsion, the curvature would also record the unobservable rotation of the example above.

  • Beyond Newton’s axioms, torsion is physical. If parallel transport of vectors other than the velocity is given physical meaning, torsion becomes observable, and its value is decided by experiment rather than by convention. Two instances:

    1. Gyroscopes. Postulating that the axis of a torque-free gyroscope is parallel transported, the torsion of the example would make it precess about the direction of motion. Experience shows that a torque-free gyroscope keeps its orientation w.r.t. non-rotating axes, i.e. is parallel transported by the torsion-free connection.

    2. Spatial metric. If \(\nabla\) is required to be compatible with a spatial metric \(h\) (Appendix B.21), Statement B applies to the spatial indices: in a Cartesian stratified chart, spatial torsion that is not totally antisymmetric in its three spatial indices changes \(\Gamma^\alpha_{(\beta\delta)}\), and hence the worldlines themselves.

    In both cases the torsion-free condition becomes a physical assumption, consistent with Newtonian experience. The definition of Newtonian spacetime thus fixes the gauge freedom left by the axioms in the one way compatible with these further observations.

10

Write the compatibility condition three times, cyclically permuting \((i, j, k)\), and add the first two minus the third. Replacing every \(\Gamma_{mab}\) by \(\Gamma_{mba} - \mathtt{T}_{mab}\) where needed, all terms except \(2 \, \Gamma_{kij}\) cancel or combine into torsion terms, which yields the expression given. Conversely, this expression satisfies the compatibility condition and has torsion \(\mathtt{T}\). \(\square\)
