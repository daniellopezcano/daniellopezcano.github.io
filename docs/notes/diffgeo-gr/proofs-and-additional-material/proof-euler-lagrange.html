B.14 Euler–Lagrange equations (§7.2.1)

Appendix B • Proofs & Comments

B.14 Euler–Lagrange equations (§7.2.1)

Setting. As in Chapter 7: \(\gamma\) is smooth, \(\textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma = \pm 1\) on \([a,b]\), \(\gamma([a,b]) \subseteq \mathcal{U}\), and \(\gamma_\mathcal{U} = \mathscr{X} \circ \gamma\). The Lagrangian \(\mathcal{L}\) is defined on the vectors over \(\mathcal{U}\) with sign \(\textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma\), \[ D \coloneqq \big\{ \mathcal{X}_p \in T\mathcal{U} \;\big|\; \textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma \, g_p(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{X}_p) > 0 \big\} \subseteq T\mathcal{U} \subseteq T\mathcal{M} . \] Writing \((\alpha \, ; \beta) \in \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d\) for the \(d\) position slots and the \(d\) velocity slots of the bundle chart \((T\mathcal{U}, \xi_\mathscr{X})\), and using \(g_p(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{X}_p) = g_{ij}(p) \, x^i_p \, x^j_p\) with \(p = \mathscr{X}^{-1}(\alpha)\) and \(x_p = \beta\), the chart image of \(D\) is \[ D_\mathscr{X} \coloneqq \xi_\mathscr{X}(D) = \Big\{ (\alpha \, ; \beta) \in \mathscr{X}(\mathcal{U}) \times \mathbb{R}^d \;\Big|\; \textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma \, g_{ij}\big( \mathscr{X}^{-1}(\alpha) \big) \, \beta^i \beta^j > 0 \Big\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d , \] and the chart representative of the Lagrangian is \begin{align*} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X} \coloneqq \mathcal{L} \circ \xi_\mathscr{X}^{-1} : D_\mathscr{X} &\longrightarrow \mathbb{R} \\ (\alpha \, ; \beta) &\longmapsto \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}(\alpha \, ; \beta) = \sqrt{ \textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma \, g_{ij}\big( \mathscr{X}^{-1}(\alpha) \big) \, \beta^i \beta^j } . \end{align*} \(D_\mathscr{X}\) is open in \(\mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d\), and \(\mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\) is smooth on it, as the square root of a smooth positive function. A point of \(D_\mathscr{X}\) is the chart description, through \(\xi_\mathscr{X}\), of a tangent vector with sign \(\textrm{\runic ᛇ}_\gamma\). For a smooth \(\textrm{\runic ᛉ} : [a,b] \to \mathbb{R}^d\) with \(\textrm{\runic ᛉ}(a) = \textrm{\runic ᛉ}(b) = 0\), the length of \(\gamma_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ}}\) is \[ L_{[a,b]}[\gamma_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ}}] = \int_a^b \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\big( \underbrace{\gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}(\lambda)}_{\text{position slots}} \, ; \, \underbrace{\dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'(\lambda)}_{\text{velocity slots}} \big) \, d\lambda , \] which makes sense as long as the argument of \(\mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\) lies in \(D_\mathscr{X}\) for all \(\lambda \in [a,b]\).

Statement. \(\gamma\) is a geodesic if and only if, for \(m = 1, \dots, d\) and all \(\lambda \in [a,b]\), \[ \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) \big) \Big] - (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) \big) = 0 . \]

Strategy. The definition of a geodesic is a condition on an integral, for every \(\textrm{\runic ᛉ}\). The proof turns it into a condition at every point \(\lambda\), in two moves:

  1. (I)

    compute the derivative at \(\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0\) (steps 1–4): \[ \frac{d}{d\textrm{\runic ᛊ}} L_{[a,b]}[\gamma_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ}}] \Big|_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} = \int_a^b \sum_{m=1}^d \left\{ \left[(\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) - \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \Big] \right] \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m(\lambda) \right\} \, d\lambda ; \]

  2. (II)

    since \(\textrm{\runic ᛉ}\) is arbitrary, this vanishes for every \(\textrm{\runic ᛉ}\) if and only if (step 5): \[ \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) \big) \Big] - (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) \big) = 0 . \]

Proof.

  1. The deformed curves are admissible for small \(|\textrm{\runic ᛊ}|\). We need \(\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}(\lambda) \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'(\lambda) \big) \in D_\mathscr{X}\) for all \(\lambda \in [a,b]\). The undeformed points form the compact set \(K \coloneqq \big\{ \big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) \big) \;\big|\; \lambda \in [a,b] \big\} \subseteq D_\mathscr{X}\). Since \(D_\mathscr{X}\) is open, there exist a \(\delta > 0\) such that \(B_\delta(k) \subseteq D_\mathscr{X}\) for every \(k \in K\), with \(B_\delta\) the open balls of the standard topology of \(\mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d = \mathbb{R}^{2d}\). The deformation keeps each point of \(K\) inside its ball as long as \[ \sum_{m=1}^d \Big[ \big( \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m(\lambda) \big)^2 + \big( \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'^m(\lambda) \big)^2 \Big] \leq \textrm{\runic ᛊ}^2 M_\textrm{\runic ᛉ}^2 < \delta^2, \qquad M_\textrm{\runic ᛉ}^2 \coloneqq \max_{\lambda \in [a,b]} \sum_{m=1}^d \Big[ \big( \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m(\lambda) \big)^2 + \big( \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'^m(\lambda) \big)^2 \Big], \] which holds for \(|\textrm{\runic ᛊ}| < \textrm{\runic ᛊ}_0 \coloneqq \delta / (1 + M_\textrm{\runic ᛉ})\). For such \(\textrm{\runic ᛊ}\) the length above is defined, and the function of two variables \[ (\textrm{\runic ᛊ}, \lambda) \longmapsto \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}(\lambda) \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'(\lambda) \big) \] is smooth on \((-\textrm{\runic ᛊ}_0, \textrm{\runic ᛊ}_0) \times [a,b]\), as a composition of smooth maps.

  2. Differentiate under the integral sign. For any \(0 < \textrm{\runic ᛊ}_1 < \textrm{\runic ᛊ}_0\), the partial derivative w.r.t. \(\textrm{\runic ᛊ}\) of the function of step 1 is continuous on the compact set \([-\textrm{\runic ᛊ}_1, \textrm{\runic ᛊ}_1] \times [a,b]\), so the derivative and the integral can be exchanged: \[ \frac{d}{d\textrm{\runic ᛊ}} L_{[a,b]}[\gamma_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ}}] \Big|_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} = \int_a^b \frac{\partial}{\partial \textrm{\runic ᛊ}} \Big[ \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}(\lambda) \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'(\lambda) \big) \Big]_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} \, d\lambda . \]

  3. Chain rule. At fixed \(\lambda\) (dropped from the notation in this step), \(\textrm{\runic ᛊ}\) enters all \(2d\) slots of \(\mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\): \begin{align*} &\frac{\partial}{\partial \textrm{\runic ᛊ}} \Big[ \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ} \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}' \big) \Big] \Big|_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} \\ \xlongrightarrow{\text{chain rule}} \; &\sum_{m=1}^d \Big[ \frac{\partial \big( \gamma^m_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m \big)}{\partial \textrm{\runic ᛊ}} \, (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ} \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}' \big) + \frac{\partial \big( \dot{\gamma}^m_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'^m \big)}{\partial \textrm{\runic ᛊ}} \, (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ} \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}' \big) \Big] \Big|_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} \\ \xlongrightarrow{\text{affine in } \textrm{\runic ᛊ}} \; &\sum_{m=1}^d \Big[ \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m \, (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ} \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}' \big) + \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'^m \, (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ} \, ; \, \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} + \textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}' \big) \Big] \Big|_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} \\ \xlongrightarrow{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} \, = \, 0} \; &\sum_{m=1}^d \Big[ \underbrace{(\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m}_{\text{position shifted by } \textrm{\runic ᛉ}} + \underbrace{(\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'^m}_{\text{velocity shifted by } \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'} \Big] . \end{align*}

  4. Integrate by parts. The functions \(\lambda \mapsto (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) \big)\) are \(C^1\) (\(\mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\) and \(\gamma\) are smooth). Inserting step 3 into step 2, \begin{align*} &\frac{d}{d\textrm{\runic ᛊ}} L_{[a,b]}[\gamma_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ}}] \Big|_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} \\ \xlongrightarrow{\text{steps 2, 3}} \; &\int_a^b \sum_{m=1}^d \Big\{ (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m + (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}'^m \Big\} d\lambda \\ \xlongrightarrow{\text{product rule}} \; &\int_a^b \sum_{m=1}^d \Big\{ (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m + \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m \Big] \\ & \qquad - \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \Big] \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m \Big\} d\lambda \\ \xlongrightarrow{\text{fund.\ theorem of calculus}} \; &\sum_{m=1}^d \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m \Big]_a^b \\ &+ \int_a^b \sum_{m=1}^d \left\{ \left[(\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) - \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \Big] \right] \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m(\lambda) \right\} \, d\lambda \\ \xlongrightarrow{\textrm{\runic ᛉ}(a) \, = \, \textrm{\runic ᛉ}(b) \, = \, 0} \; &\int_a^b \sum_{m=1}^d \left\{ \left[(\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) - \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \Big] \right] \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m(\lambda) \right\} \, d\lambda . \end{align*} The boundary term is where the fixed endpoints are used.

  5. From the integral to every point. The bracket in the integrand of step 4 is minus the left-hand side of the Statement, and is continuous in \(\lambda\) (\(\mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\) and \(\gamma\) are smooth).

    • (\(\Leftarrow\))

      If the Statement holds, the bracket vanishes for every \(m\) and \(\lambda\), so the derivative is \(0\) for every \(\textrm{\runic ᛉ}\): \(\gamma\) is a geodesic.

    • (\(\Rightarrow\))

      Suppose, on the contrary, that \(\gamma\) is a geodesic but the bracket is non-zero for some \(m\) at some \(\lambda_0 \in (a,b)\). Since the derivative of step 4 must vanish for every admissible \(\textrm{\runic ᛉ}\), it must vanish in particular for the bracket itself, multiplied by \((\lambda - a)(b - \lambda)\) so as to vanish at the endpoints: \[ \textrm{\runic ᛉ}^m(\lambda) \coloneqq (\lambda - a)(b - \lambda) \Big[ (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) - \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \Big] \Big] . \] For this choice, each term of the integrand of step 4 is \((\lambda - a)(b - \lambda) \geq 0\) times the square of the bracket. The integrand is therefore non-negative on \([a,b]\), and, by continuity, positive on an interval around \(\lambda_0\), so \[ \frac{d}{d\textrm{\runic ᛊ}} L_{[a,b]}[\gamma_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ}}] \Big|_{\textrm{\runic ᛊ} = 0} = \int_a^b \underbrace{(\lambda - a)(b - \lambda)}_{\geq \, 0} \sum_{m=1}^d \underbrace{\Big[ (\partial_m \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) - \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big[ (\partial_{d+m} \mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X})\big( \gamma_\mathcal{U} ; \dot{\gamma}_\mathcal{U} \big) \Big] \Big]^2}_{\geq \, 0, \text{ and } > \, 0 \text{ near } \lambda_0} d\lambda > 0 , \] which contradicts \(\gamma\) being a geodesic. Hence the bracket vanishes on \((a,b)\) for every \(m\), and on \([a,b]\) by continuity: this is the Statement. \(\square\)

The proof only uses that \(\mathcal{L}_\mathscr{X}\) is \(C^2\) on an open set containing \(K\). It therefore holds for the action functional \(\int_a^b \mathcal{L}\big( v_{\gamma(\lambda)} \big) \, d\lambda\) of any such Lagrangian \(\mathcal{L}\).
