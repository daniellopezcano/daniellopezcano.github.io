Appendix C • Examples

C.5 Examples of Connections, Torsion and Curvature (§§6.1–6.4)

This appendix continues the example of Appendix C.4: same manifold, same two charts, same curves, fields and scalar field. On top of it we now place three different connections, chosen so that between them they display every notion of Chapter 6:

  • \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\), flat and torsion free, whose autoparallels are straight lines in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\);

  • \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\), flat but with torsion, whose autoparallels include the circles \(\delta_{r_0}\);

  • \(\nabla^{\kappa}\), torsion free but curved, whose autoparallels are sinusoids and whose parallel transport around closed loops does not return vectors to themselves.

A connection is additional structure: nothing in \((\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}_\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{A}_{C^\infty})_2\) singles one out, and in particular the egg-carton shape of \(\mathcal{M}\) inside \(\mathbb{R}^3\) plays no role in what follows. Only once a metric is introduced will a preferred connection appear. As in the rest of the notes, the Einstein summation convention is implied, and the connection coefficient functions follow the convention of Chapter 6, \(\Gamma^i_{jk} \coloneqq dx^i\left\lceil \nabla_{\partial x_k} \partial x_j \right\rfloor\), whose last lower index labels the direction of differentiation.

C.5.1 Setting

We write functions on \(\mathcal{M}\) through the chart coordinates of their argument, \(\mathscr{X}(p) = (a, b)\) and \(\mathscr{Y}(p) = (\beta_1, \beta_2)\), related on \(\mathcal{V}\) by \[ a = \beta_1 \cos \beta_2, \qquad b = \beta_1 \sin \beta_2, \qquad \beta_1 = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} : \] expressions in \(a, b\) refer to the chart \(\mathscr{X}\), and expressions in \(\beta_1, \beta_2\) to the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\). From Appendix C.4 we only need the following objects.

  • The Jacobians of the transition maps (Subsection C.4.2), \[ \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos \beta_2 & -\beta_1 \sin \beta_2 \\ \sin \beta_2 & \beta_1 \cos \beta_2 \end{bmatrix}^s_{j}, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^l} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos \beta_2 & \sin \beta_2 \\ -\dfrac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} & \dfrac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \end{bmatrix}^i_{l}, \] and hence the relations between the chart-induced basis fields on \(\mathcal{U} \cap \mathcal{V} = \mathcal{V}\): \[ \partial y_1 = \cos \beta_2 \, \partial x_1 + \sin \beta_2 \, \partial x_2 , \qquad \partial y_2 = -\beta_1 \sin \beta_2 \, \partial x_1 + \beta_1 \cos \beta_2 \, \partial x_2 . \]

  • The curves \(\gamma_{x_0}\) and \(\delta_{r_0}\) (Subsection C.4.3), through their chart representatives, from which their velocity and acceleration components follow by differentiation: \[ \begin{array}{c@{\qquad}|@{\qquad}c} (\mathscr{X} \circ \gamma_{x_0})(\lambda) = (x_0, \, \lambda) & (\mathscr{Y} \circ \gamma_{x_0})(\lambda) = \left( \sqrt{x_0^2 + \lambda^2}, \, \operatorname{atan2}(\lambda, x_0) \right) \\[8pt] (\mathscr{X} \circ \delta_{r_0})(\lambda) = (r_0 \cos \lambda, \, r_0 \sin \lambda) & (\mathscr{Y} \circ \delta_{r_0})(\lambda) = (r_0, \, \lambda), \quad \lambda \in (-\pi, \pi) \end{array} \]

  • The vector fields \(\mathcal{X}\) and \(\mathcal{Y}\) (Subsection C.4.5), the velocity fields of the two families, whose component functions, in the notation of Section 5.3, are \[ \begin{array}{c@{\qquad}|@{\qquad}c} \left. \mathcal{X} \right|_\mathcal{U} = x^i \, \partial x_i = \partial x_2 & \left. \mathcal{X} \right|_\mathcal{V} = y^i \, \partial y_i = \sin \beta_2 \, \partial y_1 + \dfrac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 \\[12pt] \left. \mathcal{Y} \right|_\mathcal{U} = \tilde{x}^i \, \partial x_i = -b \, \partial x_1 + a \, \partial x_2 & \left. \mathcal{Y} \right|_\mathcal{V} = \tilde{y}^i \, \partial y_i = \partial y_2 \end{array} \]

  • The scalar field \(f(p) = \cos a \cos b + 1\) and its differential (Subsection C.4.4), with component functions in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) \[ \left. df \right|_\mathcal{U} = \mathtt{x}_i \, dx^i, \qquad \mathtt{x}_1 = -\sin a \cos b, \qquad \mathtt{x}_2 = -\cos a \sin b . \]

  • The second derivatives of the chart transition, needed below. By Chapter 6 they are the functions \(\frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \partial \mathscr{Y}^j}(p) = \partial_k \partial_j \left[ \mathscr{X}^s \circ \mathscr{Y}^{-1} \right]\big( \mathscr{Y}(p) \big)\), i.e. the second partial derivatives of \((\beta_1, \beta_2) \mapsto (\beta_1 \cos \beta_2, \beta_1 \sin \beta_2)\): \[ \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^1}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \partial \mathscr{Y}^j} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\sin \beta_2 \\ -\sin \beta_2 & -\beta_1 \cos \beta_2 \end{bmatrix}_{kj}, \qquad \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^2}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \partial \mathscr{Y}^j} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \cos \beta_2 \\ \cos \beta_2 & -\beta_1 \sin \beta_2 \end{bmatrix}_{kj}. \]

Finally, recall how a connection is specified in practice. By Chapter 6, on a chart domain the whole action of \(\nabla\) is fixed by the \(\dim(\mathcal{M})^3 = 8\) connection coefficient functions: for any two vector fields, \(\nabla_\mathcal{Z} \mathcal{W} = \big( \mathcal{Z}\left\lfloor w^k \right\rceil + \Gamma^k_{ji} \, w^j z^i \big) \partial x_k\). Conversely, any choice of \(8\) smooth functions on a chart domain defines, through this formula and the Leibniz rule, a connection there. Since \(\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{M}\), choosing the \(\Gamma\)’s in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) defines a connection on the whole manifold, and its coefficients in \(\mathscr{Y}\) then follow from the transformation law.

C.5.2 A flat, torsion-free connection: \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\)

Define \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\) on \(\mathcal{M}\) by declaring all its connection coefficient functions in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) to vanish, \[ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_x \right)^i_{jk} = 0, \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_{\partial x_k} \partial x_j = 0 , \] the analogue on \(\mathcal{M}\) of the familiar derivative of vector fields in the Euclidean plane, hence the label.

Coefficients in the polar chart. With \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_x = 0\), only the inhomogeneous term of the transformation law survives, \[ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^i_{jk} = \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^s} \, \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \, \partial \mathscr{Y}^j} = \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^1} \, \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^1}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \, \partial \mathscr{Y}^j} + \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^2} \, \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^2}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \, \partial \mathscr{Y}^j}, \] which is symmetric in \(j\) and \(k\), leaving six independent entries. Taking the rows of \(\partial \mathscr{Y}^i / \partial \mathscr{X}^l\) and the second derivatives listed above: \begin{align*} \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^1_{11} &= \cos \beta_2 \cdot 0 + \sin \beta_2 \cdot 0 = 0, \\ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^1_{12} = \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^1_{21} &= \cos \beta_2 \, (-\sin \beta_2) + \sin \beta_2 \, \cos \beta_2 = 0, \\ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^1_{22} &= \cos \beta_2 \, (-\beta_1 \cos \beta_2) + \sin \beta_2 \, (-\beta_1 \sin \beta_2) = -\beta_1, \\ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^2_{11} &= -\frac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} \cdot 0 + \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \cdot 0 = 0, \\ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^2_{12} = \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^2_{21} &= -\frac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, (-\sin \beta_2) + \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, \cos \beta_2 = \frac{1}{\beta_1}, \\ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y \right)^2_{22} &= -\frac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, (-\beta_1 \cos \beta_2) + \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, (-\beta_1 \sin \beta_2) = 0 . \end{align*} The same connection thus has vanishing coefficients in one chart and non-vanishing ones in the other: the \(\Gamma\)’s are not the components of a tensor field. In terms of the basis fields, \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_{\partial y_2} \partial y_2 = -\beta_1 \, \partial y_1\) and \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_{\partial y_1} \partial y_2 = \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_{\partial y_2} \partial y_1 = \frac{1}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2\), while \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_{\partial y_1} \partial y_1 = 0\).

Torsion and curvature. In the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) all \(\Gamma\)’s vanish, so \(\mathtt{T}^i_{jk} = \Gamma^i_{kj} - \Gamma^i_{jk} = 0\) and \(\mathrm{Riem}^i_{jkm} = \partial x_k\left\lfloor \Gamma^i_{jm} \right\rceil - \partial x_m\left\lfloor \Gamma^i_{jk} \right\rceil + \Gamma^i_{rk} \Gamma^r_{jm} - \Gamma^i_{rm} \Gamma^r_{jk} = 0\). Both are tensor fields, so they vanish in every chart: \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\) is flat and torsion free. It is instructive to see this happen in the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\), where the \(\Gamma\)’s do not vanish. Torsion freeness is the symmetry \((\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y)^2_{12} = (\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_y)^2_{21}\). As for the curvature, in two dimensions and thanks to the antisymmetry in the last two indices, the independent components are \(\mathrm{Riem}^i_{j12}\), and, with \(\partial y_k\) acting on functions as \(\partial / \partial \beta_k\), \begin{align*} \mathrm{Riem}^1_{212} &= \partial y_1\left\lfloor \Gamma^1_{22} \right\rceil - \partial y_2\left\lfloor \Gamma^1_{21} \right\rceil + \Gamma^1_{r1} \Gamma^r_{22} - \Gamma^1_{r2} \Gamma^r_{21} = -1 - 0 + 0 - \Gamma^1_{22} \Gamma^2_{21} = -1 + \beta_1 \cdot \frac{1}{\beta_1} = 0, \\ \mathrm{Riem}^2_{112} &= \partial y_1\left\lfloor \Gamma^2_{12} \right\rceil - \partial y_2\left\lfloor \Gamma^2_{11} \right\rceil + \Gamma^2_{r1} \Gamma^r_{12} - \Gamma^2_{r2} \Gamma^r_{11} = -\frac{1}{\beta_1^2} - 0 + \Gamma^2_{21} \Gamma^2_{12} - 0 = -\frac{1}{\beta_1^2} + \frac{1}{\beta_1^2} = 0, \end{align*} while \(\mathrm{Riem}^1_{112}\) and \(\mathrm{Riem}^2_{212}\) vanish term by term. The derivatives of the \(\Gamma\)’s and their quadratic terms cancel exactly, as they must. ✓

Covariant derivatives of the example fields. In the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) the covariant derivative reduces to the action of the vector field on the component functions. Hence \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{X} = \mathcal{Y}\left\lfloor x^k \right\rceil \partial x_k = 0, \qquad \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y} = \mathcal{X}\left\lfloor \tilde{x}^k \right\rceil \partial x_k = \partial x_2\left\lfloor -b \right\rceil \partial x_1 + \partial x_2\left\lfloor a \right\rceil \partial x_2 = -\partial x_1 , \] since the components of \(\mathcal{X}\) are constant. The same results must come out of the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\), where the components are not constant and the \(\Gamma\)’s compensate for it. With \((\nabla_\mathcal{Z} \mathcal{W})^k = \mathcal{Z}\left\lfloor w^k \right\rceil + \Gamma^k_{ji} \, w^j z^i\), \(\tilde{y}^i = (0,1)\) and \(y^i = (\sin \beta_2, \cos \beta_2 / \beta_1)\): \begin{align*} \left( \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{X} \right)^1 &= \partial y_2\left\lfloor \sin \beta_2 \right\rceil + \Gamma^1_{22} \, y^2 = \cos \beta_2 - \beta_1 \, \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} = 0, \\ \left( \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{X} \right)^2 &= \partial y_2\left\lfloor \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \right\rceil + \Gamma^2_{12} \, y^1 = -\frac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} + \frac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} = 0, \quad \checkmark \end{align*} and, since the components of \(\mathcal{Y}\) are constant in \(\mathscr{Y}\), \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y} = \Gamma^k_{2i} \, y^i \, \partial y_k = \Gamma^1_{22} \, y^2 \, \partial y_1 + \Gamma^2_{21} \, y^1 \, \partial y_2 = -\cos \beta_2 \, \partial y_1 + \frac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 , \] which, using the relations between the basis fields, is \(-\cos \beta_2 (\cos \beta_2 \, \partial x_1 + \sin \beta_2 \, \partial x_2) + \sin \beta_2 (-\sin \beta_2 \, \partial x_1 + \cos \beta_2 \, \partial x_2) = -\partial x_1\). ✓ Torsion freeness can also be checked on these two fields: their commutator is \([\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}] = [\partial x_2, -b \, \partial x_1 + a \, \partial x_2] = \partial x_2\left\lfloor -b \right\rceil \partial x_1 + \partial x_2\left\lfloor a \right\rceil \partial x_2 = -\partial x_1\), so that \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y} - \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{X} - [\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}] = -\partial x_1 - 0 + \partial x_1 = 0\). ✓

For the covector field \(df\), the formula \((\nabla_\mathcal{Z} df)_k = \mathcal{Z}\left\lfloor \mathtt{x}_k \right\rceil - \Gamma^j_{ki} \, \mathtt{x}_j \, z^i\) gives, in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\), \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{X} df = \partial x_2\left\lfloor \mathtt{x}_k \right\rceil dx^k = \sin a \sin b \; dx^1 - \cos a \cos b \; dx^2 , \] i.e. the components of \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}} df\) are the ordinary second partial derivatives of \(f \circ \mathscr{X}^{-1}\), as expected for a connection whose coefficients vanish in that chart.

Autoparallels. The autoparallel equation \(\ddot{\gamma}^k + \Gamma^k_{ji}(\gamma(\lambda)) \, \dot{\gamma}^i \dot{\gamma}^j = 0\) reads, in each chart, \[ \begin{array}{c@{\qquad}|@{\qquad}c} \text{chart } \mathscr{X}: & \text{chart } \mathscr{Y}: \\[4pt] \begin{cases} \ddot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{U} = 0 \\ \ddot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{U} = 0 \end{cases} & \begin{cases} \ddot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{V} - \gamma^1_\mathcal{V} \, \big( \dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{V} \big)^2 = 0 \\[4pt] \ddot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{V} + \dfrac{2}{\gamma^1_\mathcal{V}} \, \dot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{V} \, \dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{V} = 0 \end{cases} \end{array} \] where \(\gamma^1_\mathcal{V} \coloneqq \mathscr{Y}^1 \circ \gamma\) is the radial chart coordinate along the curve, and in the second equation both \(\Gamma^2_{12}\) and \(\Gamma^2_{21}\) contribute. In the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) the solutions are \((\mathscr{X} \circ \gamma)(\lambda) = (a_0, b_0) + \lambda \, (u, v)\): the autoparallels of \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\) are the curves whose chart representatives in \(\mathscr{X}\) are straight lines, traversed at constant speed. In particular:

  • \(\gamma_{x_0}\) is autoparallel, since \((\mathscr{X} \circ \gamma_{x_0})(\lambda) = (x_0, \lambda)\). In the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\) its representative is curved, yet it solves the corresponding equations: differentiating it, \[ \dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{V} = \left( \frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{x_0^2 + \lambda^2}}, \, \frac{x_0}{x_0^2 + \lambda^2} \right), \qquad \ddot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{V} = \left( \frac{x_0^2}{(x_0^2 + \lambda^2)^{3/2}}, \, -\frac{2 x_0 \lambda}{(x_0^2 + \lambda^2)^2} \right), \] so that \begin{align*} &\frac{x_0^2}{(x_0^2 + \lambda^2)^{3/2}} - \sqrt{x_0^2 + \lambda^2} \, \frac{x_0^2}{(x_0^2 + \lambda^2)^2} = 0, \\ &-\frac{2 x_0 \lambda}{(x_0^2 + \lambda^2)^2} + \frac{2}{\sqrt{x_0^2 + \lambda^2}} \, \frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{x_0^2 + \lambda^2}} \, \frac{x_0}{x_0^2 + \lambda^2} = 0. \quad \checkmark \end{align*} At field level, this is the statement \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{X} = \mathcal{X}\left\lfloor x^k \right\rceil \partial x_k = 0\).

  • \(\delta_{r_0}\) is not autoparallel. Its acceleration, computed in either chart, \begin{align*} \text{chart } \mathscr{X}: \quad a_{\delta}(\lambda) &= \ddot{\delta}^k_\mathcal{U} \, \partial x_k = -r_0 \cos \lambda \, \partial x_1 - r_0 \sin \lambda \, \partial x_2, \\ \text{chart } \mathscr{Y}: \quad a_{\delta}(\lambda) &= \big( 0 + \Gamma^1_{22} \cdot 1 \big) \partial y_1 + \big( 0 + 2 \, \Gamma^2_{12} \cdot 0 \cdot 1 \big) \partial y_2 = -r_0 \, \partial y_1 , \end{align*} is the same vector (use \(\partial y_1 = \cos \lambda \, \partial x_1 + \sin \lambda \, \partial x_2\) along the curve), pointing towards the origin. Since it is not proportional to the velocity \(\partial y_2\), not even a reparametrization of \(\delta_{r_0}\) is autoparallel. At field level, \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{Y} = -\beta_1 \, \partial y_1 \neq 0\).

Parallel transport. Let \(\mathcal{Z}\) be a vector field along a curve \(\gamma\) with components \(z^k(\lambda)\) in a chart. By the same argument used for the autoparallel equation, \((\nabla_{v_{\gamma(\lambda)}} \mathcal{Z})^k = \dot{z}^k + \Gamma^k_{ji}(\gamma(\lambda)) \, z^j \, \dot{\gamma}^i\), so parallel transport along \(\gamma\) is the linear system of ODEs \[ \dot{z}^k(\lambda) + \Gamma^k_{ji}\big( \gamma(\lambda) \big) \, z^j(\lambda) \, \dot{\gamma}^i(\lambda) = 0 . \] For \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\) in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) this is \(\dot{z}^k = 0\): parallel transport keeps the cartesian components constant, along any curve. Hence \(\mathcal{X}\) is parallel along every curve, whereas \(\mathcal{Y}\) is parallel along none of our curves, e.g. \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y} = -\partial x_1 \neq 0\) along \(\gamma_{x_0}\). Transporting an initial vector with cartesian components \((c_1, c_2)\) along \(\delta_{r_0}\), where \(\dot{\delta}^i_\mathcal{V} = (0,1)\) and \(\beta_1 = r_0\), the polar components must instead solve \[ \dot{z}^1 + \Gamma^1_{22} \, z^2 = \dot{z}^1 - r_0 \, z^2 = 0, \qquad \dot{z}^2 + \Gamma^2_{12} \, z^1 = \dot{z}^2 + \frac{1}{r_0} \, z^1 = 0, \] whose solution is \(z^1(\lambda) = c_1 \cos \lambda + c_2 \sin \lambda\), \(z^2(\lambda) = (-c_1 \sin \lambda + c_2 \cos \lambda) / r_0\). Converting back with \(\textrm{\runic ᛞ}_{\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V}}\) gives \(\cos \lambda \, z^1 - r_0 \sin \lambda \, z^2 = c_1\) and \(\sin \lambda \, z^1 + r_0 \cos \lambda \, z^2 = c_2\). ✓ The transported vector keeps its cartesian direction while its polar components rotate. After a full turn it returns to itself, as it must for a flat connection.

C.5.3 A flat connection with torsion: \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\)

The second connection is built by declaring a frame, rather than a chart, to be parallel. On \(\mathcal{M}^* \coloneqq \mathcal{M} \setminus \{ (0, 0, h(0,0)) \}\), i.e. where \(a^2 + b^2 > 0\), consider the unit radial and angular vector fields \[ e_1 \coloneqq \frac{a \, \partial x_1 + b \, \partial x_2}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}, \qquad e_2 \coloneqq \frac{-b \, \partial x_1 + a \, \partial x_2}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}, \] which on \(\mathcal{V}\) read \(e_1 = \partial y_1\) and \(e_2 = \frac{1}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2\). They form a frame at every point of \(\mathcal{M}^*\), but, unlike \(\{ \partial x_i \}\) or \(\{ \partial y_i \}\), not a chart-induced one. Define \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\) by \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{Z} \, e_a = 0 \qquad \forall \, \mathcal{Z} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}^*, \; a = 1, 2 . \] By the product rule this fixes the connection completely: any vector field is \(\mathcal{W} = w^a e_a\) with \(w^a \in C^\infty(\mathcal{M}^*)\), and then \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{Z} \mathcal{W} = \mathcal{Z}\left\lfloor w^a \right\rceil e_a\). A connection of this kind, which makes a global frame parallel, is called a teleparallel (or Weitzenböck) connection, hence the label.

Coefficients in the polar chart. Since \(\partial y_1 = e_1\) and \(\partial y_2 = \beta_1 \, e_2\), the product rule gives \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial y_k} \partial y_1 = 0, \qquad \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial y_k} \partial y_2 = \partial y_k\left\lfloor \mathscr{Y}^1 \right\rceil e_2 = \delta^1_k \, e_2 = \frac{\delta^1_k}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 , \] so that the only non-vanishing coefficient function is \[ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_y \right)^2_{21} = \frac{1}{\beta_1} \qquad \big( \text{while } \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_y \right)^2_{12} = 0 \big). \]

Coefficients in the cartesian chart. Inverting the definition of the frame, \[ \partial x_1 = \frac{a \, e_1 - b \, e_2}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}, \qquad \partial x_2 = \frac{b \, e_1 + a \, e_2}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}, \] and the product rule, with the frame fields parallel, gives \begin{align*} \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial x_k} \partial x_1 &= \partial x_k\left\lfloor \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil e_1 - \partial x_k\left\lfloor \frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil e_2, \\ \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial x_k} \partial x_2 &= \partial x_k\left\lfloor \frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil e_1 + \partial x_k\left\lfloor \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil e_2 . \end{align*} The four derivatives needed are \begin{gather*} \partial x_1\left\lfloor \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil = \frac{b^2}{(a^2 + b^2)^{3/2}}, \qquad \partial x_2\left\lfloor \frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil = \frac{a^2}{(a^2 + b^2)^{3/2}}, \\ \partial x_2\left\lfloor \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil = \partial x_1\left\lfloor \frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} \right\rceil = -\frac{a b}{(a^2 + b^2)^{3/2}}, \end{gather*} and, re-expressing \(b \, e_1 + a \, e_2 = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \; \partial x_2\) and \(a \, e_1 - b \, e_2 = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \; \partial x_1\), \begin{align*} \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial x_1} \partial x_1 &= \frac{b \, \big( b \, e_1 + a \, e_2 \big)}{(a^2 + b^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{b}{a^2 + b^2} \, \partial x_2, & \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial x_2} \partial x_1 &= -\frac{a \, \big( b \, e_1 + a \, e_2 \big)}{(a^2 + b^2)^{3/2}} = -\frac{a}{a^2 + b^2} \, \partial x_2, \\ \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial x_1} \partial x_2 &= -\frac{b \, \big( a \, e_1 - b \, e_2 \big)}{(a^2 + b^2)^{3/2}} = -\frac{b}{a^2 + b^2} \, \partial x_1, & \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{\partial x_2} \partial x_2 &= \frac{a \, \big( a \, e_1 - b \, e_2 \big)}{(a^2 + b^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{a}{a^2 + b^2} \, \partial x_1, \end{align*} i.e. the non-vanishing coefficient functions in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) are \[ \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_x \right)^2_{11} = \frac{b}{a^2 + b^2}, \qquad \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_x \right)^2_{12} = -\frac{a}{a^2 + b^2}, \qquad \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_x \right)^1_{21} = -\frac{b}{a^2 + b^2}, \qquad \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_x \right)^1_{22} = \frac{a}{a^2 + b^2} . \]

The two connections differ by a tensor field. Although neither set of \(\Gamma\)’s is tensorial, their difference is: the inhomogeneous terms, which depend only on the charts, cancel in the transformation law. In the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\), the difference \(D \coloneqq \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}} - \Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}\) has components \[ D^1_{22} = 0 - (-\beta_1) = \beta_1, \qquad D^2_{12} = 0 - \frac{1}{\beta_1} = -\frac{1}{\beta_1}, \qquad D^2_{21} = \frac{1}{\beta_1} - \frac{1}{\beta_1} = 0, \] and, since \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_x = 0\), its components in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) must be exactly \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_x\). Checking one of them with the \((1,2)\)-tensor transformation law, \begin{align*} D^1_{22}\big|_\mathscr{X} &= \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^1}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^l} \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^s}{\partial \mathscr{X}^2} \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^p}{\partial \mathscr{X}^2} \, D^l_{sp} = \cos \beta_2 \left( \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \right)^2 \beta_1 + \left( -\beta_1 \sin \beta_2 \right) \sin \beta_2 \, \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \left( -\frac{1}{\beta_1} \right) \\ &= \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1}, \end{align*} which is indeed \(\left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_x \right)^1_{22} = a / (a^2 + b^2)\) written in the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\), since \(a = \beta_1 \cos \beta_2\) and \(a^2 + b^2 = \beta_1^2\). ✓Note also that \(D^i_{j1} = 0\): the two connections agree when differentiating along \(\partial y_1\), i.e. radially, and differ only in the angular direction.

Torsion. In the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\), \(\mathtt{T}^2_{12} = \Gamma^2_{21} - \Gamma^2_{12} = \frac{1}{\beta_1} = -\mathtt{T}^2_{21}\), all other components vanishing, i.e. \(\mathtt{T}(\cdot, \partial y_1, \partial y_2) = \frac{1}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2\). In the chart \(\mathscr{X}\), \[ \mathtt{T}^1_{12} = \Gamma^1_{21} - \Gamma^1_{12} = -\frac{b}{a^2 + b^2}, \qquad \mathtt{T}^2_{12} = \Gamma^2_{21} - \Gamma^2_{12} = \frac{a}{a^2 + b^2}, \] i.e. \(\mathtt{T}(\cdot, \partial x_1, \partial x_2) = \big( -b \, \partial x_1 + a \, \partial x_2 \big) / (a^2 + b^2) = \mathcal{Y} / (a^2 + b^2)\). Both results describe the same tensor field: since \(\partial y_j = \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \, \partial x_s\), bilinearity and antisymmetry give \[ \mathtt{T}(\cdot, \partial y_1, \partial y_2) = \det\!\left( \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \right) \mathtt{T}(\cdot, \partial x_1, \partial x_2) = \beta_1 \cdot \frac{1}{\beta_1^2} \, \partial y_2 = \frac{1}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 , \] using \(\mathcal{Y} = \partial y_2\) and \(a^2 + b^2 = \beta_1^2\). ✓ The origin of the torsion is transparent in the frame: since \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}} e_a = 0\), \[ \mathtt{T}(\cdot, e_1, e_2) = -[e_1, e_2] = -\Big[ \partial y_1, \frac{1}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 \Big] = -\partial y_1\left\lfloor \frac{1}{\mathscr{Y}^1} \right\rceil \partial y_2 = \frac{1}{\beta_1^2} \, \partial y_2 = \frac{1}{\beta_1} \, e_2 : \] a connection that declares a non-chart-induced frame parallel has a torsion measuring how much the frame fields fail to commute. As a last check, take our two fields in the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\), where, in terms of the frame, \(\mathcal{X} = \sin \beta_2 \, \partial y_1 + \frac{\cos \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 = \sin \beta_2 \, e_1 + \cos \beta_2 \, e_2\) and \(\mathcal{Y} = \partial y_2 = \beta_1 \, e_2\). Since the frame is parallel, only the frame components get differentiated: \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y} = \mathcal{X}\left\lfloor \mathscr{Y}^1 \right\rceil e_2 = \sin \beta_2 \, e_2, \qquad \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{X} = \partial y_2\left\lfloor \sin \beta_2 \right\rceil e_1 + \partial y_2\left\lfloor \cos \beta_2 \right\rceil e_2 = \cos \beta_2 \, e_1 - \sin \beta_2 \, e_2 , \] where \(\mathcal{X}\left\lfloor \mathscr{Y}^1 \right\rceil = y^1 = \sin \beta_2\). Their commutator, \([\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}] = -\partial x_1 = -\cos \beta_2 \, \partial y_1 + \frac{\sin \beta_2}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 = -\cos \beta_2 \, e_1 + \sin \beta_2 \, e_2\), was computed in Subsection C.5.2, so that \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y} - \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{X} - [\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}] = \sin \beta_2 \, e_2 - \cos \beta_2 \, e_1 + \sin \beta_2 \, e_2 + \cos \beta_2 \, e_1 - \sin \beta_2 \, e_2 = \sin \beta_2 \, e_2 , \] in agreement with \(\mathtt{T}(\cdot, \mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}) = y^1 \, \mathtt{T}(\cdot, \partial y_1, \partial y_2) = \sin \beta_2 \, \frac{1}{\beta_1} \, \partial y_2 = \sin \beta_2 \, e_2\), the term with \(y^2\) dropping out by antisymmetry. ✓

Curvature. Since the frame is parallel, \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{X} \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{Y} e_a - \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{Y} \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{X} e_a - \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_{[\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}]} e_a = 0\) for \(a = 1, 2\), and by \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M}^*)\)-linearity in the slot of \(\mathcal{Z}\), \(\mathrm{Riem} = 0\): \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\) is flat. In the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\) this is immediate: every term of \(\mathrm{Riem}^i_{j12} = \partial y_1\left\lfloor \Gamma^i_{j2} \right\rceil - \partial y_2\left\lfloor \Gamma^i_{j1} \right\rceil + \Gamma^i_{r1} \Gamma^r_{j2} - \Gamma^i_{r2} \Gamma^r_{j1}\) contains either a coefficient with last index \(2\), all of which vanish, or the derivative \(\partial y_2\left\lfloor \Gamma^i_{j1} \right\rceil\), which vanishes since \(\Gamma^2_{21} = 1/\beta_1\) does not depend on \(\beta_2\). Curvature and torsion are thus independent notions: \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\) and \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\) are both flat, and only the latter has torsion.

A covariant derivative compared. For the same scalar field \(f\) and direction \(\mathcal{X} = \partial x_2\), the two connections give different covariant derivatives of \(df\). In the chart \(\mathscr{X}\), with \(x^i = (0,1)\), \((\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{X} df)_k = \partial x_2\left\lfloor \mathtt{x}_k \right\rceil - \left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_x \right)^j_{k2} \mathtt{x}_j\), so that \[ \nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{X} df = \left( \sin a \sin b - \frac{a \cos a \sin b}{a^2 + b^2} \right) dx^1 + \left( -\cos a \cos b + \frac{a \sin a \cos b}{a^2 + b^2} \right) dx^2 , \] which differs from \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}_\mathcal{X} df\) by the terms coming from \(D\).

Autoparallels. In the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\), the autoparallel equation of \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\) contains a single \(\Gamma\), with \(j = 2\), \(i = 1\): \[ \ddot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{V} = 0, \qquad \ddot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{V} + \frac{1}{\gamma^1_\mathcal{V}} \, \dot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{V} \, \dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{V} = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{d}{d\lambda} \Big( \gamma^1_\mathcal{V} \, \dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{V} \Big) = 0 . \] Hence \(\gamma^1_\mathcal{V}(\lambda) = r_0 + u \lambda\) and \(\gamma^1_\mathcal{V} \dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{V} = w\) constant, and the autoparallels are:

  • for \(u = 0\), \(\gamma^2_\mathcal{V}(\lambda) = \varphi_0 + (w / r_0) \, \lambda\): circles traversed at constant speed. In particular \(\delta_{r_0}\), with \(\dot{\delta}^i_\mathcal{V} = (0,1)\), is autoparallel for \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\), while it was not for \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\). At field level, \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}_\mathcal{Y} \mathcal{Y} = \partial y_2\left\lfloor \mathscr{Y}^1 \right\rceil e_2 = 0\), since the radial coordinate is constant along the circles;

  • for \(w = 0\), radial lines, \(\gamma^2_\mathcal{V} = \varphi_0\), the only curves that are autoparallel for both connections, in agreement with \(D^i_{j1} = 0\);

  • for \(u, w \neq 0\), \(\gamma^2_\mathcal{V}(\lambda) = \varphi_0 + \frac{w}{u} \ln\!\left( 1 + \frac{u \lambda}{r_0} \right)\), i.e. \(\gamma^1_\mathcal{V} = r_0 \, e^{u (\gamma^2_\mathcal{V} - \varphi_0) / w}\): logarithmic spirals.

The vertical curves \(\gamma_{x_0}\), \(x_0 \neq 0\), are no longer autoparallel: \(\ddot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{V} = x_0^2 / (x_0^2 + \lambda^2)^{3/2} \neq 0\). Note that the autoparallel equation only involves the symmetric part of the \(\Gamma\)’s: the torsion-free connection with the same symmetric part as \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\) has exactly the same autoparallels. Likewise, the normal coordinates of Chapter 6 can remove the symmetric part \(\left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_y \right)^2_{(12)} = \frac{1}{2\beta_1}\) at any given point, but no chart can remove the antisymmetric part \(\left( \Gamma^{\mathrm{W}}_y \right)^2_{[21]} = \frac{1}{2\beta_1}\), which is fixed by the torsion.

Parallel transport. By construction, \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) are parallel along every curve, and a vector field is parallel along \(\gamma\) iff its frame components \(w^a\) are constant along it. Along \(\delta_{r_0}\), \(\dot{\delta}^i_\mathcal{V} = (0,1)\) and all \(\Gamma^k_{j2}\) vanish, so the parallel transport equations reduce to \(\dot{z}^k = 0\) in the chart \(\mathscr{Y}\): parallel transport along the circles keeps the polar components constant. In the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) the transported vector, \(\big( z^1 \cos \lambda - r_0 z^2 \sin \lambda, \; z^1 \sin \lambda + r_0 z^2 \cos \lambda \big)\), co-rotates with the circle, in sharp contrast with \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\). In particular \(\mathcal{Y} = \partial y_2\) is parallel along the circles, whereas \(\mathcal{X}\), with polar components \((\sin \lambda, \cos \lambda / r_0)\) along \(\delta_{r_0}\), is not.

C.5.4 A curved, torsion-free connection: \(\nabla^{\kappa}\)

Neither of the previous connections is curved. Fix a constant \(\kappa > 0\) and define \(\nabla^{\kappa}\) on \(\mathcal{M}\) by a single non-vanishing coefficient function in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\), \[ \left( \Gamma^{\kappa}_x \right)^1_{22} = \kappa \, a, \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad \nabla^{\kappa}_{\partial x_2} \partial x_2 = \kappa \, a \, \partial x_1, \quad \nabla^{\kappa}_{\partial x_1} \partial x_1 = \nabla^{\kappa}_{\partial x_1} \partial x_2 = \nabla^{\kappa}_{\partial x_2} \partial x_1 = 0 . \]

Torsion and curvature. The coefficients are trivially symmetric in their lower indices, so \(\nabla^{\kappa}\) is torsion free. In the Riemann curvature, every quadratic term \(\Gamma^i_{rk} \Gamma^r_{jm}\) can only be non-zero for \(r = 1\), and then contains a vanishing factor \(\Gamma^i_{1k}\); the only non-vanishing derivative is \(\partial x_1\left\lfloor \Gamma^1_{22} \right\rceil = \kappa\). Hence the only non-vanishing components are \[ \mathrm{Riem}^1_{212} = \partial x_1\left\lfloor \Gamma^1_{22} \right\rceil - \partial x_2\left\lfloor \Gamma^1_{21} \right\rceil = \kappa = -\mathrm{Riem}^1_{221}, \] and the Ricci curvature, \(\mathrm{Ric}_{ab} = \mathrm{Riem}^m_{amb}\), has the single component \(\mathrm{Ric}_{22} = \mathrm{Riem}^1_{212} = \kappa\), i.e. \(\mathrm{Ric} = \kappa \, dx^2 \otimes dx^2\). Being a tensor field that does not vanish in one chart, the curvature cannot be made to vanish in any other: no choice of chart makes all the \(\Gamma\)’s of \(\nabla^\kappa\) vanish on an open set.

Autoparallels. The autoparallel equation in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) reads \[ \ddot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{U} + \kappa \, \gamma^1_\mathcal{U} \, \big( \dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{U} \big)^2 = 0, \qquad \ddot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{U} = 0 . \] The second equation gives \(\gamma^2_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) = b_0 + v \lambda\), and the first then becomes the harmonic oscillator \(\ddot{\gamma}^1_\mathcal{U} + \kappa v^2 \gamma^1_\mathcal{U} = 0\), so that \[ \gamma^1_\mathcal{U}(\lambda) = A \cos\!\big( \sqrt{\kappa} \, v \lambda + \psi \big) \quad \longrightarrow \quad a = A \cos\!\big( \sqrt{\kappa} \, (b - b_0) + \psi \big) : \] the autoparallels of \(\nabla^\kappa\) are sinusoids in the chart \(\mathscr{X}\), all with the same wave number \(\sqrt{\kappa}\), independently of the speed \(v\). Among our curves, only \(\gamma_0\) (\(A = 0\)) is autoparallel; for \(x_0 \neq 0\), \(\gamma_{x_0}\) has acceleration \(\nabla^\kappa_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{X} = \nabla^\kappa_{\partial x_2} \partial x_2 = \kappa \, x_0 \, \partial x_1\) along it.

Parallel transport around a loop. In the chart \(\mathscr{X}\) the parallel transport equations along a curve with velocity components \(\dot{\gamma}^i_\mathcal{U}\) are \(\dot{z}^1 + \kappa \, \gamma^1_\mathcal{U} \, z^2 \, \dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{U} = 0\) and \(\dot{z}^2 = 0\). Transport a vector with components \((z^1, z^2)\) counterclockwise around the rectangle \([x_0, x_1] \times [b_0, b_0 + \Delta b]\):

  • along the two horizontal sides \(\dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{U} = 0\), so nothing changes;

  • up the right side, \(a = x_1\) and \(\dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{U} = 1\), so \(z^1\) changes by \(-\kappa \, x_1 \, z^2 \, \Delta b\);

  • down the left side, \(a = x_0\) and \(\dot{\gamma}^2_\mathcal{U} = -1\), so \(z^1\) changes by \(+\kappa \, x_0 \, z^2 \, \Delta b\).

After the full loop, \[ \Delta z^1 = -\kappa \, z^2 \, (x_1 - x_0) \, \Delta b = -\mathrm{Riem}^1_{212} \, z^2 \times \text{(area enclosed)}, \qquad \Delta z^2 = 0 : \] the vector does not return to itself, and the mismatch is proportional to the curvature component, to the transported vector and to the enclosed area. Repeating the computation for \(\nabla^{\mathrm{E}}\) or \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\) gives \(\Delta z = 0\) for every loop, as expected for flat connections (for \(\nabla^{\mathrm{W}}\), loops must avoid the origin).
