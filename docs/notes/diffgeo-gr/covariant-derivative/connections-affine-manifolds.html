6.1 Connections and Affine Manifolds

Chapter 6 • Covariant Derivative

6.1 Connections and Affine Manifolds

  • A (linear) connection \(\nabla\) on a smooth manifold \((\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{A})\) assigns to every tangent vector \(\mathcal{X}_p \in T_p\mathcal{M}\), at any point \(p \in \mathcal{M}\), and to every tensor field \(T \in \Gamma T^{(r,s)}\mathcal{M}\), of any valence \((r,s)\), a tensor at \(p\), \begin{align*} \nabla : T_p\mathcal{M} \times \Gamma T^{(r,s)}\mathcal{M} &\longrightarrow T_p^{(r,s)}\mathcal{M} \\ (\mathcal{X}_p, T) &\longmapsto \nabla(\mathcal{X}_p, T) \coloneqq \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T, \end{align*} called the covariant derivative of \(T\) along \(\mathcal{X}_p\)1, satisfying, for all \(\mathcal{X}_p, \mathcal{Y}_p \in T_p\mathcal{M}\), \(\alpha \in \mathbb{R}\), \(f \in C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\) and \(T_1, T_2 \in \Gamma T^{(r,s)}\mathcal{M}\):

    1. Action on scalar fields: \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} f = \mathcal{X}_p(f)\).

    2. Additivity in the tensor field: \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} (T_1 \oplus_\Gamma T_2) = \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T_1 \oplus_\Gamma \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T_2\).

    3. Leibniz rule: using as an example a \((1,1)\)-tensor field \(T\), for all \(\mathsf{X} \in \Gamma T^*\mathcal{M}\) and \(\mathcal{Y} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}\), \[ \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \big( T(\mathsf{X}, \mathcal{Y}) \big) = (\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T)(\mathsf{X}_p, \mathcal{Y}_p) \oplus_\Gamma T_p(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X}, \mathcal{Y}_p) \oplus_\Gamma T_p(\mathsf{X}_p, \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y}), \] where the left-hand side is the covariant derivative of the scalar field \(T(\mathsf{X}, \mathcal{Y})\), and analogously for tensor fields of any valence, with one term per argument.

    4. linearity in the direction: \(\nabla_{\alpha \mathcal{X}_p + \mathcal{Y}_p} T = \alpha \, \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T \oplus_\Gamma \nabla_{\mathcal{Y}_p} T\).

    5. Smoothness: for every vector field \(\mathcal{X} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}\), the map \(p \mapsto \nabla_{\mathcal{X}(p)} T\) is a smooth tensor field, denoted \(\nabla_\mathcal{X} T \in \Gamma T^{(r,s)}\mathcal{M}\).

    The two arguments play different roles. The direction enters only through a single tangent vector at \(p\), whereas the tensor field is genuinely differentiated: \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T\) depends on the values of \(T\) around \(p\). Letting the direction vary with a vector field, property 5 yields the field-level map \begin{align*} \nabla : \Gamma T\mathcal{M} \times \Gamma T^{(r,s)}\mathcal{M} &\longrightarrow \Gamma T^{(r,s)}\mathcal{M} \\ (\mathcal{X}, T) &\longmapsto \nabla_\mathcal{X} T, \end{align*} which, applying property 4 at each point with \(\alpha = f(p)\), is \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-linear in the vector field, \[ \nabla_{f \odot_\Gamma \mathcal{X} \oplus_\Gamma \mathcal{Y}} T = f \odot_\Gamma \nabla_{\mathcal{X}} T \oplus_\Gamma \nabla_{\mathcal{Y}} T \qquad \forall \, f \in C^\infty(\mathcal{M}), \; \mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}. \] Many texts take this field-level map as the definition, with \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-linearity as an axiom. Both definitions are equivalent2, but the pointwise one is the natural one for differentiating along curves, where the direction is the velocity of the curve, only available at the points of the curve. From now onwards, sums of fields and their s-multiplication by scalar fields are written as \(+\) and juxtaposition (instead of \(\oplus_\Gamma\), \(\odot_\Gamma\) and their starred counterparts)

  • An affine manifold is the quadruple \((\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{A}, \nabla)\), i.e. a smooth manifold equipped with a connection.

  • Product rule. Properties 1 and 3 imply, for all \(g \in C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\) and \(\mathcal{Y} \in \Gamma T\mathcal{M}\), \[ \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} (g \, \mathcal{Y}) = \mathcal{X}_p(g) \, \mathcal{Y}_p + g(p) \, \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y}, \] and likewise \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p}(g \, T) = \mathcal{X}_p(g) \, T_p + g(p) \, \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T\) for tensor fields of any valence. Indeed, regarding \(\mathcal{Y}\) as a \((1,0)\)-tensor field, \(\mathcal{Y}(\mathsf{X}) \coloneqq \mathsf{X}\left\lceil \mathcal{Y} \right\rfloor\), the Leibniz rule reads \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \big( \mathcal{Y}(\mathsf{X}) \big) = (\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y})(\mathsf{X}_p) + \mathcal{Y}_p(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X})\). Applying it to \(g\,\mathcal{Y}\), and evaluating the left-hand side with property 1 and the product rule of tangent vectors acting on products of scalar fields3, \begin{align*} \big( \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p}(g\,\mathcal{Y}) \big)(\mathsf{X}_p) + g(p) \, \mathcal{Y}_p(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X}) &= \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \big( g \, \mathcal{Y}(\mathsf{X}) \big) = \mathcal{X}_p(g) \, \mathcal{Y}_p(\mathsf{X}_p) + g(p) \, \mathcal{X}_p\big( \mathcal{Y}(\mathsf{X}) \big) \\ &= \mathcal{X}_p(g) \, \mathcal{Y}_p(\mathsf{X}_p) + g(p) \, (\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y})(\mathsf{X}_p) + g(p) \, \mathcal{Y}_p(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X}). \end{align*} The terms \(g(p) \, \mathcal{Y}_p(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X})\) cancel and, since every covector at \(p\) is the value of some covector field (Section 4.2), the product rule follows.

1

In this text, the terms covariant derivative and connection are used interchangeably. In these notes the connection is the whole structure \(\nabla\), while the covariant derivative is what it produces, \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T\). In a more general setting, however, a connection is a strictly more general notion than a covariant derivative; see Appendix B.9.

2

\(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-linearity implies that \((\nabla_\mathcal{X} T)(p)\) depends on \(\mathcal{X}\) only through \(\mathcal{X}(p)\): in a chart, \(\left. \mathcal{X} \right|_\mathcal{U} = x^i \, \partial x_i\) gives \((\nabla_\mathcal{X} T)(p) = x^i(p) \, (\nabla_{\partial x_i} T)(p)\). Hence \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T \coloneqq (\nabla_\mathcal{X} T)(p)\), for any \(\mathcal{X}\) with \(\mathcal{X}(p) = \mathcal{X}_p\), is well defined.

3

\(\mathcal{X}_p(g h) = \mathcal{X}_p(g) \, h(p) + g(p) \, \mathcal{X}_p(h)\), which follows from the product rule of ordinary derivatives applied to \(\big( (g h) \circ \gamma \big)'(\lambda_0)\).
