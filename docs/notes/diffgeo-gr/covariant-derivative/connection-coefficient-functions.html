6.2 Connection Coefficient Functions

Chapter 6 • Covariant Derivative

6.2 Connection Coefficient Functions

  • Let \((\mathcal{U}, \mathscr{X})\) be a chart. Since a connection is local4, it also acts on tensor fields defined only over \(\mathcal{U}\), such as \(\partial x_j\) and \(dx^j\). To determine the freedom left by the axioms, consider \(\mathcal{X}_p = x^i_p \, (\partial x_i)_p\) and \(\left. \mathcal{Y} \right|_\mathcal{U} = \tilde{x}^j \, \partial x_j\). Property 4 and the product rule give \[ \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y} = x^i_p \, \nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} \big( \tilde{x}^j \, \partial x_j \big) = \mathcal{X}_p(\tilde{x}^j) \, (\partial x_j)_p + x^i_p \, \tilde{x}^j(p) \, \nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} \partial x_j . \] The first term is fixed by the axioms. The only data left free are the vectors \(\nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} \partial x_j \in T_p\mathcal{M}\), whose components define the connection coefficient functions of \(\nabla\) w.r.t. the chart \((\mathcal{U}, \mathscr{X})\): \begin{align*} \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk} : \mathcal{U} &\longrightarrow \mathbb{R} \\ p &\longmapsto \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk}(p) \coloneqq (dx^i)_p\big( \nabla_{(\partial x_k)_p} \partial x_j \big), \end{align*} i.e. \(\nabla_{(\partial x_k)_p} \partial x_j = \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk}(p) \, (\partial x_i)_p\), or, at field level, \(\nabla_{\partial x_k} \partial x_j = \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk} \, \partial x_i\). Note the index convention: the last lower index labels the direction of differentiation. The components of \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y}\) therefore read \[ \left( \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y} \right)^k \coloneqq (dx^k)_p\big( \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathcal{Y} \big) = \mathcal{X}_p(\tilde{x}^k) + \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^k_{ji}(p) \, \tilde{x}^j(p) \, x^i_p , \] or, letting the direction vary with a vector field \(\left. \mathcal{X} \right|_\mathcal{U} = x^i \, \partial x_i\), \(\left( \nabla_\mathcal{X} \mathcal{Y} \right)^k = \mathcal{X}\left\lfloor \tilde{x}^k \right\rceil + \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^k_{ji} \, \tilde{x}^j \, x^i\). The \(\dim(\mathcal{M})^3\) functions \(\left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk}\) thus fully determine the action of \(\nabla\) on vector fields over \(\mathcal{U}\).

  • Analogously, for a covector field \(\left. \mathsf{X} \right|_\mathcal{U} = \mathtt{x}_j \, dx^j\), \[ \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X} = x^i_p \, \nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} \big( \mathtt{x}_j \, dx^j \big) = \mathcal{X}_p(\mathtt{x}_j) \, (dx^j)_p + x^i_p \, \mathtt{x}_j(p) \, \nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} dx^j , \] where the data left free are now the covectors \(\nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} dx^j \in T_p^*\mathcal{M}\), with components \begin{align*} \big(\tilde{\Gamma}_\mathscr{X}\big)^i_{jk} : \mathcal{U} &\longrightarrow \mathbb{R} \\ p &\longmapsto \big(\tilde{\Gamma}_\mathscr{X}\big)^i_{jk}(p) \coloneqq \big( \nabla_{(\partial x_k)_p} dx^i \big)\big( (\partial x_j)_p \big), \end{align*} i.e. \(\nabla_{\partial x_k} dx^i = \big(\tilde{\Gamma}_\mathscr{X}\big)^i_{jk} \, dx^j\). These, however, are not independent of the \(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\)’s. Applying the Leibniz rule to the constant functions \(dx^j\left\lceil \partial x_k \right\rfloor = \delta^j_k\), whose covariant derivative vanishes by property 1, \[ 0 = \nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p}\big( dx^j \left\lceil \partial x_k \right\rfloor \big) = \big( \nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} dx^j \big)\big( (\partial x_k)_p \big) + (dx^j)_p\big( \nabla_{(\partial x_i)_p} \partial x_k \big) = \big(\tilde{\Gamma}_\mathscr{X}\big)^j_{ki}(p) + \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^j_{ki}(p), \] so that \(\tilde{\Gamma}_\mathscr{X} = -\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\), and the components of \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X}\) read \[ \left( \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X} \right)_k \coloneqq \left( \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} \mathsf{X} \right)\big( (\partial x_k)_p \big) = \mathcal{X}_p(\mathtt{x}_k) - \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^j_{ki}(p) \, \mathtt{x}_j(p) \, x^i_p . \] Repeated use of the Leibniz rule gives the action of \(\nabla\) on tensor fields of any valence: every upper index contributes a \(+\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\) term, and every lower index a \(-\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\) term. For instance, for a \((1,1)\)-tensor field with components \(T^i_j\), \[ \left( \nabla_\mathcal{X} T \right)^i_{j} = \mathcal{X}\left\lfloor T^i_{j} \right\rceil + \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{m\ell} \, T^m_{j} \, x^\ell - \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^m_{j\ell} \, T^i_{m} \, x^\ell. \]

  • Let us now derive how the connection coefficient functions transform under a change of chart. On \(\mathcal{U} \cap \mathcal{V}\), with the transformation laws of Section 5.3, \(dy^i = \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^l} \, dx^l\) and \(\partial y_j = \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \, \partial x_s\), the \(C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\)-linearity in the direction and the product rule give \begin{align*} \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{Y}\right)^i_{jk} &\coloneqq dy^i\left\lceil \nabla_{\partial y_k} \partial y_j \right\rfloor = \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^l} \, \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^p}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k} \, dx^l\left\lceil \nabla_{\partial x_p} \left( \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \, \partial x_s \right) \right\rfloor \\ &= \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^l} \, \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^p}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k} \, dx^l\left\lceil \partial x_p\left\lfloor \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \right\rceil \partial x_s + \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \, \nabla_{\partial x_p} \partial x_s \right\rfloor \\ &= \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^s} \, \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^p}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k} \, \partial x_p}_{\partial y_k} \left\lfloor \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \right\rceil + \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^l} \, \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^p}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k} \, \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \, \underbrace{dx^l\left\lceil \nabla_{\partial x_p} \partial x_s \right\rfloor}_{\left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^l_{sp}}, \end{align*} where we used \(dx^l\left\lceil \partial x_s \right\rfloor = \delta^l_s\). The first term contains the basis field \(\partial y_k\) acting on the smooth function \(\frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} : p \mapsto \partial_j \left[ \mathscr{X}^s \circ \mathscr{Y}^{-1} \right] \big( \mathscr{Y}(p) \big)\). Since \(\frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \circ \mathscr{Y}^{-1} = \partial_j \left[ \mathscr{X}^s \circ \mathscr{Y}^{-1} \right]\), the definition of the chart-induced basis gives \[ \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \, \partial \mathscr{Y}^j}(p) \coloneqq \partial y_k\left\lfloor \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \right\rceil (p) = \partial_k \partial_j \left[ \mathscr{X}^s \circ \mathscr{Y}^{-1} \right] \big( \mathscr{Y}(p) \big), \] an ordinary second partial derivative of the chart transition map \(\textrm{\runic ᛖ}_{\mathcal{V}\mathcal{U}}\), which is smooth since the atlas is \(C^\infty\)-compatible, and symmetric in \(j\) and \(k\) by Schwarz’s theorem. We thus arrive at the transformation law \[ \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{Y}\right)^i_{jk} = \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^s} \, \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k \, \partial \mathscr{Y}^j} + \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^l} \, \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^s}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \, \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^p}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^k} \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^l_{sp}. \] The second term is exactly the transformation law of the components of a \((1,2)\)-tensor field. The first one, involving second derivatives of the chart transition map, shows that the connection coefficient functions are not the components of a tensor field.

  • Normal coordinates. The inhomogeneous term can be exploited to simplify the connection coefficient functions at a chosen point. Let \(p \in \mathcal{U}\) and assume, w.l.o.g. (translating the chart), \(\mathscr{X}(p) = (0, \dots, 0)\). Define a new chart \((\mathcal{V}, \mathscr{Y})\) around \(p\) through the transition map \[ \left( \mathscr{Y}^i \circ \mathscr{X}^{-1} \right)(\alpha^1, \dots, \alpha^d) \coloneqq \alpha^i + \frac{1}{2} \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{(jk)}(p) \, \alpha^j \alpha^k, \qquad \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{(jk)} \coloneqq \frac{1}{2} \Big[ \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk} + \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{kj} \Big], \] which, by the inverse function theorem (the Jacobian of this transition map at \(\mathscr{X}(p) = 0\) is the identity), defines a chart on some neighbourhood \(\mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{U}\) of \(p\), \(C^\infty\)-compatible with every chart of \(\mathcal{A}\). It therefore belongs to the maximal atlas determined by \(\mathcal{A}\) (see the footnote to the definition of a smooth manifold in Chapter 3), which is the atlas we always tacitly work with5. Its first and second derivatives at \(p\) are \[ \left( \frac{\partial \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^j} \right)_p = \delta^i_j = \left( \frac{\partial \mathscr{X}^i}{\partial \mathscr{Y}^j} \right)_p, \qquad \left( \frac{\partial^2 \mathscr{Y}^i}{\partial \mathscr{X}^k \, \partial \mathscr{X}^j} \right)_p = \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{(jk)}(p). \] Writing the transformation law with the roles of the two charts exchanged, and evaluating it at \(p\), \[ \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk}(p) = \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{(jk)}(p) + \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{Y}\right)^i_{jk}(p) \, \longrightarrow \, \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{Y}\right)^i_{jk}(p) = \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{[jk]}(p) \coloneqq \frac{1}{2} \Big[ \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{jk}(p) - \left(\Gamma_\mathscr{X}\right)^i_{kj}(p) \Big]. \] The symmetric part of the connection coefficient functions can thus always be made to vanish at any chosen point, whereas the antisymmetric part cannot: it is, up to a factor \(-1/2\), the torsion introduced below, a tensor field, which vanishes in one chart if and only if it vanishes in all of them. Note also that this holds at the single point \(p\) only: the derivatives of the \(\Gamma\)’s cannot, in general, be removed, which is related to the curvature introduced below.

4

If \(T\) vanishes on an open neighbourhood \(\mathcal{W}\) of \(p\), then \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T = 0\). Indeed, take \(\psi \in C^\infty(\mathcal{M})\) with \(\psi(p) = 0\) and \(\psi = 1\) outside a compact subset of \(\mathcal{W}\) (one minus a bump function around \(p\)). Then \(T = \psi \, T\), and the product rule gives \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T = \mathcal{X}_p(\psi) \, T_p + \psi(p) \, \nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T = 0\), since \(T_p = 0\) and \(\psi(p) = 0\). Hence \(\nabla_{\mathcal{X}_p} T\) only depends on \(T\) near \(p\).

5

In much of the literature, “normal coordinates” refers to the coordinates built from the autoparallels through \(p\) (the exponential map). The quadratic construction used here is the minimal one achieving \(\left(\Gamma_\mathscr{Y}\right)^i_{(jk)}(p) = 0\), which is all we need.
